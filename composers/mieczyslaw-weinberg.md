# Mieczysław Weinberg (1919-1996)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two things: the child's song that darkens (the Children's Songs op. 13), and the solo line over a bare accompaniment (the Clarinet Sonata op. 28). **Scores:** none read. His music is under copyright; this page is from seven sources, and what they say is said here in other words.

**Measured profile:** none: no work of his is in score-reader's catalogue.

**The pieces that used this page,** with Philip's words on each: composer-blend, [`reference/composers/mieczyslaw-weinberg.md`](https://github.com/phgz/composer-blend/blob/main/reference/composers/mieczyslaw-weinberg.md).

## Techniques

1. **A wordless song at the start, and the same song at the end, sadder.** The Children's Songs open with an introduction sung without words; the coda repeats its material in a much sadder tone, after the cycle has gone from four light, playful songs to a child's grief at a home destroyed. [MusicWeb; Oxford Song]
2. **From light to darkness,** by sharp contrast: lyrical and scherzo-like songs against tragic ones; the war through a child's eyes. [Gnesin]
3. **New tunes in an old mode, not quotations.** Both cycles of Jewish songs use the mode of the Sabbath prayers (*Adonoy Molokh*); he quotes no synagogue chant but makes melodies the way the tradition makes its variants. [mus.academy]
4. **Not really childish:** spiky energy and angular lines under the children's words. [MusicWeb]
5. **A simple singing tune at the start, low and soft,** on the clarinet; the piano answers with a playful, wandering tune over a rather mechanical bass. The piano stays bare and plain all through. [Uniarts]
6. **A dance tune over an even, plain accompaniment,** with the seconds and minor thirds of klezmer, odd trills, grace notes like sobs, and wailing lines. [Uniarts]
7. **The folk manner is pushed until it turns:** the grace notes and trills come more and more often, the half-step figures insist, and at the height what was carefree is desperate. [Uniarts]
8. **The stuck record:** at that height the piano repeats one fragment so stubbornly that it sounds like a machine. [Uniarts]
9. **Then it calms, the opening comes back soft, and the last chord is major.** All three movements of the sonata end on a major chord, two of them after sorrow. [Uniarts]
10. **A solo that plays alone:** the slow movement opens with the piano alone, then the clarinet alone, broken only by a few deep chords. He also wrote twenty-four preludes for a cello alone. [Uniarts; Wikipedia]
11. **Clarinet and violin** are the solo voices he favours in orchestral works; laments in the slow music, klezmer in the lively. [BBC Music Magazine]
12. **Clear home notes:** his music mostly has strongly marked tonal centres; harsher from the late 1960s. [BBC Music Magazine]
13. **Plucked strings:** his Piano Quintet lost a prize because a juror objected to its plucking. [Wikipedia]
14. **Music for children and for the circus and cartoons:** a ballet from a children's show, the songs of the Soviet *Winnie-the-Pooh*, much circus music; lullabies with a rocking motion all through (op. 110). [Wikipedia; MusicWeb]

## Numbers

- **Clarinet Sonata op. 28**: about twenty minutes, three movements: *Allegro*, *Allegretto*, *Adagio*; each ends on a major chord. [Uniarts]
- **Children's Songs op. 13** (1943, poems by Peretz): a wordless introduction, four light songs, a song of grief, and a coda on the introduction's material, sadder. [MusicWeb; Oxford Song]

## What he does not do

*By these sources.*

- Quote folk or synagogue tunes: he writes his own in their manner.
- Fill the accompaniment: it stays bare.
- End in the minor after a sorrowful movement (in the clarinet sonata).
