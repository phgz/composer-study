# William Grant Still (1895-1978)

## Techniques

### The blues symphony (Symphony no. 1, *Afro-American*)

1. **One blues melody as the theme of the whole symphony.** He used a melody in the blues style as the basic theme, and it appears in each movement; its melodic essence pervades the symphony.
   *Works on:* form, melody.
   *Why:* in his notes of 1931, the blues are more purely Black in character than many spirituals; he wanted music recognisable as in the idiom of Black Americans, and in 1964 said he had had no programme in mind. He portrays "the sons of the soil", not the "New Negro". [Bard; Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony; BSO] *(his words)*
   *How:* write one tune in the blues form and let every section take it or something grown from it: as itself, as a counter-melody, as a second theme's source, transformed in mood.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, all four movements. [Bard; BSO]
2. **An invocation, then the blues on a muted trumpet, answered.** After a brief invocation on the cor anglais, a muted trumpet languidly outlines a "textbook" blues theme over the twelve-bar blues pattern, answered in call and response by horns and woodwinds.
   *Works on:* melody, colour, form.
   *How:* a few bars of a solo cor anglais; then the tune on a muted trumpet, in twelve bars, slow; after each of its short phrases, let horns and woodwinds answer in the gap.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, first movement, the opening. [BSO; Hollywood Bowl]
3. **The strophe again on another instrument, the flutes cutting in straight.** The theme returns at bar 19 on the clarinet; the flutes interrupt each of its small phrases with a counter-melody in straight, unswung rhythm. A third strophe goes to energetic strings.
   *Works on:* texture, rhythm, colour.
   *How:* repeat the twelve-bar strophe with the tune on a new instrument each time; fill the gaps after its phrases with a figure in even notes against the tune's lilt; give the last strophe to the strings, livelier.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, first movement, from bar 19. [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony; BSO]
4. **A second theme grown from the first, like a spiritual.** At bar 45 the oboe brings a second theme in G major, developed from motifs of the first: a sweeping arc, gentle syncopation, reminiscent of a spiritual; it later returns in the cellos; touches of vibraphone colour the subsidiary music.
   *Works on:* melody, colour.
   *How:* take two or three motifs of the blues tune and rebuild them into a broader, arching, hymn-like line for the oboe, in another key; colour it with vibraphone.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, first movement, from bar 45. [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony; Hollywood Bowl; BSO]
5. **The second theme's tail grown into a loud march.** Its ending motifs expand into a loud, march-like development with snare drum, very rhythmic; it leads back to the second theme in the minor, more lamenting, and the first theme returns to end the movement.
   *Works on:* form, rhythm, loudness.
   *How:* take the last notes of a theme, repeat them in a march rhythm over a snare drum, growing loud; then let the theme come back in the minor, sadder.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, first movement, the development. [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony]
6. **A slow movement on one tonal centre.** The slow movement stays on an invariant tonal centre, unusual in a symphony but usual in the blues; it is more chromatic, its chords less functional, and it reuses the first movement's themes in the manner of a spiritual, with dark colours.
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *How:* stay on one keynote throughout; let chromatic chords move over it without leading to other keys.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, second movement ("Sorrow"). [BSO; Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony; Hollywood Bowl]
7. **The scherzo: the banjo, a theme with a horn counter-melody, a fanfare that interrupts.** A propulsive scherzo with the twang of the banjo, a sound of very early jazz recordings; its theme ("Hallelujah", from bar 8) has a horn counter-melody (bars 8 to 11) that resembles Gershwin's "I Got Rhythm", a melodic shape Still had favoured before; flute and oboe take it next (bars 12 to 15); a fanfare-like theme in the low strings and winds interrupts the first whenever it appears.
   *Works on:* colour, rhythm, form.
   *Why:* the banjo is there for local colour, in a movement heard as a "leap for joy"; one historian (Catherine Parsons Smith, cited by the BSO's note) hears Still playing the trickster in it, behind the minstrel mask. [Hollywood Bowl; BSO] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* a quick movement with a plucked banjo chording; under the tune, a horn line in short syncopated notes; a loud fanfare in the low instruments breaking in, now and then.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, third movement ("Humor"). [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony; BSO; Hollywood Bowl]
8. **A finale without the dominant's pull.** The last movement opens like a hymn, modal, with no modulation and no authentic cadence, leaving the keynote uncertain; its slow modal sections centre on E; the finale's bass moves from F to D flat.
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *How:* write the hymn with modal chords and avoid every dominant-to-tonic close; let a bass step down a major third for the last turn.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, fourth movement. [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony]
9. **The blues tune returning in the finale, quicker, merging, a fiery coda.** The finale is close to a rondo: its main theme blues-based, its second a version of the first movement's blues melody, varied, then back in a quicker episode near the end where it merges with the main theme's return; a reminiscence of the original blues theme in a fiery coda.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* bring the first tune back at the end, faster, overlapping with the finale's own theme until the two are one; end loud on a short memory of the first tune.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 1, fourth movement. [Listen; Hollywood Bowl]
10. **Choirs alternating for colour.** One of his trademarks here is a skilful use of orchestral colour, especially by alternating the choirs of strings, woodwinds and brass.
    *Works on:* colour.
    *How:* pass the tune and its answers from one family of instruments to another, rather than mixing them.
    *Where:* Symphony no. 1. [Listen]
11. **Melody and harmony from the blues scale, syncopated.** The melodies and harmonies are built from the scale of blues and jazz musicians rather than the diatonic scale; the rhythm is syncopated; the key and tonic are always clear.
    *Works on:* melody, harmony, rhythm.
    *How:* lower the third and seventh (and use both forms of the third against each other); accent off the beat; keep the keynote plain.
    *Where:* Symphony no. 1. [Listen]

### The string quartet (*Danzas de Panamá*, 1948)

12. **The strings as drums.** In "Tamborito", the first of the four dances (on Panamanian tunes collected by Elisabeth Waldo), the players strike the sides of their instruments to make the rhythm, before a highly chromatic introduction; the last, "Cumbia y Congo", begins with percussive hand-pounding.
    *Works on:* rhythm, colour.
    *How:* before the tune, the string players knock a dance rhythm on the bodies of their instruments; then they bow.
    *Where:* *Danzas de Panamá*, "Tamborito" and "Cumbia y Congo". [Edition Silvertrust, via the LA Phil]

13. **The dances' other colours.** "Mejorana y Socavón" is "a sweet and upbeat Panamanian waltz"; in "Punto" there is "the clear influence of Mexican music in the rhythms and arpeggio figures"; "Cumbia y Congo" begins with percussive hand-pounding, "at first, it sounds purely African but very quickly a heavy dose of Latin melody is added". The first and last dances are African in origin, the middle two of Spanish and Indian descent. With strings only, he approximated the sounds of the native instruments, among them the three-stringed violin, the *rabel*, and a dance of the shoes, the *zapateo*.
    *Works on:* rhythm, colour, melody.
    *How:* let each dance be one colour of the region: a waltz; arpeggio figures in a Mexican rhythm; a drummed start to which a Latin tune is added; imitate a folk fiddle or the stamp of shoes with the quartet's own means.
    *Where:* *Danzas de Panamá*, all four. [a programme note by "NCS" on audienceaccess.co; Hollywood Bowl; Edition Silvertrust; Max Derrickson's note, musicprogramnotes.com]

### The *Lyric Quartette* (1960)

14. **Three portraits of friends.** First titled "Musical Portraits of Three Friends": "The Sentimental One", "The Quiet One", "The Jovial One"; then retitled "On a Plantation", "In the Mountains of Peru" ("Based on an Inca melody", written in both sets of titles), "In a Pioneer Settlement". Dedicated to the Hollywood String Quartet's violinist Joachim Chassman. About six, five and a half and three minutes.
    *Works on:* form, background.
    *How:* a quartet in three short movements, each a character: a warm one, a gentle one on a borrowed tune, an energetic one last and shortest.
    *Where:* the *Lyric Quartette*. [All Classical Portland's research page; Edition Silvertrust via earsense.org; earsense.org's recordings list]
15. **Each movement opens in unison.** "The four instruments begin in rhythmic unison, then expand into a busier texture," anchored by occasional pizzicato ("The Quiet One"); the first movement too opens in "unison fluctuations", the ensemble playing "as a unit of sound".
    *Works on:* texture.
    *How:* begin a movement with all four in one rhythm, as one instrument, then let them part into a busier texture, with a pizzicato now and then to hold it down.
    *Where:* *Lyric Quartette*, first and second movements. [Princeton University Concerts' note; Alex Burns, classicalexburns.com]
16. **French colour, then jazz.** The first movement "mostly calm and genial with touches of melancholy", with "a vague aura of French Impressionism"; the second "gentle with echoes of Debussy"; the third "full of energy, is loud and boisterous, with elements of jazz".
    *Works on:* colour, harmony.
    *How:* write the calm movements in Debussy's harmonic colours and keep the jazz for the last, the loud one.
    *Where:* *Lyric Quartette*. [Edition Silvertrust via earsense.org]
17. **His words of 1950.** "There is resentment against a Negro composer who doggedly insists that he can and will write abstract music of a non-racial nature"; and if he writes racial music, his opponents will say white composers did it better (quoted in Princeton University Concerts' note, which sets them against Dvořák's call of 1893 for an American music from "the Negro melodies or Indian chants": see Dvořák's page, 20).
    *Works on:* background.
    *How:* (a background entry, no recipe).
    *Where:* (his words, not a work). [Princeton University Concerts]

## Walks through whole works

**Symphony no. 1, *Afro-American*** (about 24 minutes; four movements, each with a line of Paul Laurence Dunbar and a title from his notebook). I, "Longing" (*Moderato assai*), in a loose sonata form: the cor anglais's invocation; the blues on the muted trumpet, answered by horns and woodwinds (2); again on the clarinet at bar 19, the flutes cutting in (3); a third strophe in the strings; the oboe's second theme at bar 45 (4); the march (5); the second theme in the minor; the blues to close. II, "Sorrow" (*Adagio*): one tonal centre, the first movement's themes as a spiritual (6). III, "Humor" (*Animato*), in A flat: the banjo, "Hallelujah" with the horn's counter-melody, the fanfare (7). IV, "Aspiration" (*Lento, con risoluzione*): a hymn without dominant cadences (8), the blues back in a quicker episode, merged, a fiery coda (9). [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony; BSO; Hollywood Bowl; Listen]

## Numbers

- **Symphony no. 1:** about 24 minutes; three flutes (the third with piccolo), two oboes, cor anglais, three clarinets, bass clarinet, two bassoons, four horns, three trumpets, three trombones, tuba; timpani; vibraphone, triangle, wire brush, small cymbal, cymbals, snare drum, bass drum, wood block, orchestra bells, gong; harp, celesta, tenor banjo; strings. Composed in about three months while he had no steady work, from sketches begun in 1924. [Wikipedia, Afro-American Symphony]

## What he does not do

*In the blues symphony.*

- **No atonal music:** the key and tonic are always clear [Listen].
- **No programme:** in 1964 he said he had had none in mind [Bard].
