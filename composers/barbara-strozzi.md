# Barbara Strozzi (1619-1677)

Begun by Claude on 24 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 70, and extended the same day for piece 70b. It grows each time she is used.

**Scope so far.** One kind of piece: the aria for solo voice and continuo built on a bass that repeats, as *Che si può fare* (from the *Arie* op. 8, her last collection, 1664) shows it, with the chromatic walk of *Tu me ne puoi*. **Scores:** yes. score-reader's catalogue (version 25) holds 28 of her works, all from CPDL: arias, cantatas and madrigals. The two named above were read by measurement from Philip's ready-read copy; the 28 were searched for a repeating bass. *Che si può fare* is CPDL's "modernized" edition, so its note values may not be the print's. Each technique marked **[score]** names the bars of the reading.

**Measured profile:** [`profiles/barbara-strozzi.md`](../profiles/barbara-strozzi.md), generated from score-reader's catalogue by `study/profiles.py`: her airs and her madrigals against other composers'.

**The pieces that used this page,** with Philip's words on each: composer-blend, `reference/composers/barbara-strozzi.md` (piece 70: 4.25, "Yes, 4.25 for 70"; piece 70b: 3.75, "It sounds ok, but not as moving as the other").

## Techniques

### The aria over a walking bass

1. **A bass that walks down four notes and starts again.** In *Che si può fare* the bass goes E, D, C, B, each note six beats long, and starts again on E, 28 times without a break: the first 678 of the aria's 1,241 beats, a little over half.
   *Works on:* harmony, form, rhythm.
   *How:* write a bass that steps down from the keynote to the fifth below in long, equal notes, and repeat it unchanged while the voice moves over it.
   *Where:* *Che si può fare*, bars 1 to 47. **[score: Che si può fare]** A bass that falls a fourth from keynote to fifth and repeats is what is called a lament bass [Wikipedia, Lament bass].

2. **The voice stops in the middle of the walk.** Of the 19 places where the voice rests for two beats or more inside a cycle of the bass, 14 fall on the walk's second or third note (D or C), two on its first.
   *Works on:* form, melody.
   *Why:* the bass never starts again together with a phrase of the voice: her silences fall while it is still walking, and its new start comes under her singing. **[score: Che si può fare]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* end the singer's phrases on the second or third note of the walk, so that the bass carries on through her rest and begins again under her next phrase.
   *Where:* *Che si può fare*, bars 1 to 47. **[score: Che si può fare]**

3. **Over the walk's last note, both D sharp and D.** Over B, the bottom of the walk, the voice sings D sharp for 23 beats and D natural for 16; over E she sings C sharp (23 beats) rather than C. Over the walk, she is on a dissonance with the bass (a second, fourth, tritone or seventh) for a fifth of her time (21%). Her first long phrase falls from E to B by way of D sharp and C sharp (bar 3).
   *Works on:* harmony, melody.
   *How:* over the walk's bottom note let the third be sometimes major, sometimes minor; over the keynote, raise the sixth; leave the voice on a dissonance with the bass for about a fifth of the time.
   *Where:* *Che si può fare*, bars 1 to 47; the first phrase, bars 2 to 4. **[score: Che si può fare]**

4. **The walk by half steps, the voice climbing against it.** In *Tu me ne puoi* the bass descends C, B, B flat, A, A flat, G in long notes (bars 71 to 74) and begins again at bar 75; above it the voice rises E flat, F, G, then from C to E flat.
   *Works on:* harmony, melody.
   *How:* fill the bass's falling fourth with every half step, slowly, and let the voice rise by step while it falls.
   *Where:* *Tu me ne puoi*, bars 71 to 76. **[score: Tu me ne puoi]** The chromatic form of the lament bass fills in every half step of the fourth [Wikipedia, Lament bass].

5. **Small turns around a half step, and few leaps.** Measured against 149 airs by 40 other composers: a turn up a half step, down a half step, down a step, its last note much longer, 3.1 times in 1,000 four-note figures against 0.2, in six of her airs; a leap followed by a step back 43% of the time against 29%; leaps of a fourth or more 11% of her moves against 17%.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* move mostly by step; after a leap, step back; let a phrase turn around a half step before falling to a long note.
   *Where:* *Che si può fare*, bar 74; *Non c'è più fede*, bar 3; *Con male nuove*, bar 16. **[score: her airs, the profile]**

6. **A strongly metred, smooth line with its dissonances placed with care.** A note on *Che si può fare* hears the aria's dissonances handled carefully inside a smooth lyricism and a strong sense of metre, and the voice often ornamented, with melismas and repetition; the aria has ritornellos between its vocal sections.
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm, background.
   *How:* not a technique on its own: it says that the dissonances of 3 sit inside a regular, singable metre.
   *Where:* *Che si può fare*. [USC, Che si può fare]

### The quick aria in six-eight

7. **A lilting dotted figure.** In *E pazzo il mio core*, a quick aria whose bars hold six eighths, the voice sings a dotted eighth, a sixteenth and an eighth 17 times in 303 notes; *Luci belle* 13 times, *Chi brama in Amore* 9. Over it the bass goes in a quarter and an eighth, or a dotted quarter.
   *Works on:* rhythm, melody.
   *How:* in six-eight, give the voice the dotted eighth, sixteenth and eighth again and again, over a bass in quarter and eighth.
   *Where:* *E pazzo il mio core*, bars 6 to 12. **[score: E pazzo il mio core]**

## Walks through whole works

***Che si può fare*** (op. 8, 1664 [USC]; 138 bars and 1,241 beats as read). Bars 1 to 47: the walking bass, 28 times, the voice over it (techniques 1 to 3). Bars 47 to 99: a freer section, the bass in quick notes (mostly sixteenths and half notes), no repeat. Bars 99 to 133: a third section, its bass in quarters and eighths; the walk is heard once at its start, in quick notes. Bars 133 to 138: the walk once more, quickly, and a close. The voice ranges from E flat 4 to A5. **[score: Che si può fare]**

## Numbers

- **The catalogue:** 28 works (version 25). Of the 28, only *Che si può fare* repeats its bass more than twice in a row; *Tu me ne puoi* repeats its chromatic walk three times; *L'amante modesto* repeats one bass note fifteen times. **[score]**
- **Che si può fare:** the walking bass 678 of 1,241 beats; each bass note six beats; the voice E flat 4 to A5. **[score: Che si può fare]**

## What she does not do

*In the 28 works of the catalogue.*

- Build a whole piece on a repeating bass: even *Che si può fare* leaves its walk after 47 bars. **[score]**
