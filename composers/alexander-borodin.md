## Techniques

1. **The cello sings first, high, and alone has the tune.** For the first 23 bars the first violin is silent; the cello has the tune between B3 and A4, marked solo, singing and expressive. **[score: Notturno]**
2. **A long note, a quick turn, a long note.** The tune's bars go: a note of two beats; then three or four quick notes circling the next one (a triplet, or four sixteenths going back and forth) and a note of two beats again. Six in ten of its moves are steps; nearly three in ten are leaps of a fourth or more, most of them a fall of a fourth and back (E, B, E). **[score: Notturno]**
3. **The accompaniment does not sit on the beat.** Second violin and viola strike on the bar line, then on each half-beat, tied over the beats: an eighth, a quarter, a quarter, an eighth, two of them to a bar in turn. Seven in ten of their notes begin off the beat. **[score: Notturno]**
4. **A held note under it.** The viola keeps one note (the keynote) while the second violin's thirds move. **[score: Notturno]**
5. **The tune again, two octaves higher,** by the first violin from bar 25, the cello now a bass. **[score: Notturno]**
6. **The middle is quicker and made of a rising scale:** a run of sixteenths up an octave and a half, then a falling figure with a repeated note and a short turn, said four times, each time lower. **[score: Notturno]**
7. **The tune comes back in canon at one beat.** Bars 112 to 130: the cello leads and the first violin follows one beat later, an octave higher. Bars 134 to 154: the first violin leads and the second follows one beat later, an octave lower. **[score: Notturno]**
8. **It ends on one high held note,** the keynote, in the first violin (A6) and the cello (A5, an octave above where it sang). **[score: Notturno]**
9. **Two tunes heard apart, then together.** *In the Steppes of Central Asia* has a Russian song and an Eastern melody, ornamented, on the cor anglais, with a plucked figure for the caravan's steps; near the end the two tunes sound at once, each keeping its shape; at the close only the Russian one is left, fading. [Wikipedia, Steppes]
10. **Grace notes and trills on the singing line:** 54 grace notes in the first violin's part and 42 in the cello's; trills in both violins. The viola and second violin shake their notes (tremolo) for long stretches. **[score: Notturno]**

## Walks through whole works

**String Quartet no. 2, third movement, Notturno** (180 bars, 3/4, A major, *Andante*). From the score, by bar numbers and by which part has the tune; what the middle does in detail was not followed.
- **Bars 1 to 23:** the cello alone has the tune, high in its range (B3 to A4), marked solo, singing; second violin and viola keep the off-beat accompaniment over the viola's held A. The first violin is silent (techniques 1 to 4).
- **Bars 24 to 47:** the first violin has the tune two octaves higher; the cello becomes the bass. It dies away (*dim.* at bar 43, *rall.* at bar 46) (technique 5).
- **From bar 48, quicker** (*più mosso*): the rising run of sixteenths and the falling figure said four times, each lower (technique 6); louder from bar 63, softer from bars 74 and 81.
- **Bars 112 to 130:** the tune in canon, the cello leading and the first violin one beat behind, an octave higher. **Bars 134 to 154:** the canon again, the first violin leading and the second following, an octave lower (technique 7).
- **The last bars:** one high held note, the keynote, in the first violin (A6) and the cello (A5) (technique 8).

## What he does not do

*In the Notturno.*

- Give the tune to two instruments at once in unison: it is one voice, or a canon.
- Put the accompaniment's chords on the beat under the tune.
- End loud, or with a cadence played out: the last thing is a held note.
