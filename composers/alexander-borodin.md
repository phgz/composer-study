# Alexander Borodin (1833-1887)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

## Techniques

1. **The cello sings first, high, and alone has the tune.** For the first 23 bars the first violin is silent; the cello has the tune between B3 and A4, marked solo, singing and expressive.
   *Works on:* texture, colour.
   *How:* give the first statement of the tune to the cello in the top of its range (a sixth or a seventh between its lowest and highest notes), and keep the highest instrument silent until the tune is over, so that the cello is the top voice.
   *Where:* Notturno, bars 1 to 23. **[score: Notturno]**
2. **A long note, a quick turn, a long note.** The tune's bars go: a note of two beats; then three or four quick notes circling the next one (a triplet, or four sixteenths going back and forth) and a note of two beats again. Six in ten of its moves are steps; nearly three in ten are leaps of a fourth or more, most of them a fall of a fourth and back (E, B, E).
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm.
   *How:* in 3/4, write bars of a half note and a quarter; fill the quarter before a long note with a triplet or four sixteenths that turn around the coming note (above, the note, above). Every third or fourth bar, put a fall of a fourth and back in place of the turn.
   *Where:* Notturno, the cello's bars 1 to 8 (bar 2 has the triplet, bar 3 the four sixteenths, bar 4 the fall of a fourth and back). **[score: Notturno]**
3. **The accompaniment does not sit on the beat.** Second violin and viola strike on the bar line, then on each half-beat, tied over the beats: an eighth, a quarter, a quarter, an eighth, two of them to a bar in turn. Seven in ten of their notes begin off the beat.
   *Works on:* rhythm, texture.
   *How:* under the tune, give the chord to two players who strike an eighth on the downbeat and then quarters that start on the half-beats and are tied over the beats, ending with an eighth. Nothing strikes on beats two and three except the tune.
   *Where:* Notturno, second violin and viola, bars 1 to 23 and wherever the tune returns. **[score: Notturno]**
4. **A held note under it.** The viola keeps one note (the keynote) while the second violin's thirds move.
   *Works on:* harmony, texture.
   *How:* let one of the two accompanying players repeat a single note, the keynote or the fifth, in the off-beat rhythm of technique 3, while the other moves in thirds with the harmony.
   *Where:* Notturno, bars 1 to 2: the viola on A3 under the second violin's C sharp and E, then B and D. **[score: Notturno]**
5. **The tune again, two octaves higher,** by the first violin from bar 24, the cello now a bass.
   *Works on:* form, colour.
   *How:* repeat the whole tune unchanged two octaves up on the highest instrument; move the instrument that sang it to the bass, and keep the accompaniment of technique 3 between them.
   *Where:* Notturno, bars 24 to 47. **[score: Notturno]**
6. **The middle is quicker and made of a rising scale:** a run of sixteenths up an octave and a half, then a falling figure with a repeated note and a short turn, said four times, each time lower.
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm, form.
   *How:* after the tune's second statement, quicken the speed; start the middle with one scale in even sixteenths over an octave and a half, then a two-bar figure (a note struck twice, a step down, a short turn) said four times, each a step lower.
   *Where:* Notturno, first violin from bar 48 (*più mosso*): the scale in bars 48 and 49, the falling figure from bar 50. **[score: Notturno]**
7. **The tune comes back in canon at one beat.** Bars 112 to 130: the cello leads and the first violin follows one beat later, an octave higher. Bars 134 to 154: the first violin leads and the second follows one beat later, an octave lower.
   *Works on:* texture, form.
   *How:* when the tune returns, give it to two instruments at once, the second entering one beat after the first at the octave, with the same notes and lengths. Then let the follower lead and a third instrument follow.
   *Where:* Notturno, bars 112 to 130 (cello, then first violin) and 134 to 154 (first violin, then second). **[score: Notturno]**
8. **It ends on one high held note,** the keynote, in the first violin (A6) and the cello (A5, an octave above where it sang).
   *Works on:* form, colour.
   *How:* close on the keynote held for two bars or more, high in two instruments an octave apart, with no chord struck under it.
   *Where:* Notturno, the last bars (177 to 180). **[score: Notturno]**
9. **Two tunes heard apart, then together.** *In the Steppes of Central Asia* has a Russian song and an Eastern melody, ornamented, on the cor anglais, with a plucked figure for the caravan's steps; near the end the two tunes sound at once, each keeping its shape; at the close only the Russian one is left, fading.
   *Works on:* melody, texture, form.
   *How:* write two tunes of different character that also fit together note against note; give each its own instrument and its own section, with a plucked walking figure under both; near the end sound them together unchanged, then drop one and let the other fade.
   *Where:* *In the Steppes of Central Asia*: the Russian song first, the cor anglais's melody after it, the two together near the end. The source gives no bar numbers. [Wikipedia, Steppes]
10. **Grace notes and trills on the singing line:** 54 grace notes in the first violin's part and 42 in the cello's; trills in both violins. The viola and second violin shake their notes (tremolo) for long stretches.
    *Works on:* melody, colour.
    *How:* give the singing line a grace note before some of its long notes and a trill on one now and then; let the accompaniment change from the off-beat rhythm to tremolo for a whole passage.
    *Where:* Notturno, the first violin's and the cello's parts throughout; the tremolo is on 177 of the viola's notes and 91 of the second violin's, in passages that were not located bar by bar. **[score: Notturno]**

## Walks through whole works

**String Quartet no. 2, third movement, Notturno** (180 bars, 3/4, A major, *Andante*). From the score, by bar numbers and by which part has the tune; what the middle does in detail was not followed.
- **Bars 1 to 23:** the cello alone has the tune, high in its range (B3 to A4), marked solo, singing; second violin and viola keep the off-beat accompaniment over the viola's held A. The first violin is silent (techniques 1 to 4).
- **Bars 24 to 47:** the first violin has the tune two octaves higher; the cello becomes the bass. It dies away (*dim.* at bar 43, *rall.* at bar 46) (technique 5).
- **From bar 48, quicker** (*più mosso*): the rising run of sixteenths and the falling figure said four times, each lower (technique 6); louder from bar 63, softer from bars 74 and 81.
- **Bars 112 to 130:** the tune in canon, the cello leading and the first violin one beat behind, an octave higher. **Bars 134 to 154:** the canon again, the first violin leading and the second following, an octave lower (technique 7).
- **The last bars:** one high held note, the keynote, in the first violin (A6) and the cello (A5) (technique 8).

## What he does not do

*In the Notturno.*

- Give the tune to two instruments at once in unison: it is one voice, or a canon.
- Put the accompaniment's chords on the beat under the tune.
- End loud, or with a cadence played out: the last thing is a held note.
