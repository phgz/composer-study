## Techniques

### The trio sonatas: the harmony

1. **The circle of fifths drives the harmony.** A source calls the circle of fifths the main driver of his chord progressions. In the 149 labelled movements the commonest move from one chord's root to the next is a fall of a fifth: 32% of all changes, against 14% up a step and 11% up a fifth (23% keep the root).
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *How:* let most chords move to the chord a fifth below (or a fourth above); use the step up and the fifth up much more rarely.
   *Where:* the trio sonatas, opp. 1 to 4. [Wikipedia, Arcangelo Corelli] **[score: the 149 DCML movements]**
2. **A chain of sevenths falling by fifths.** Runs of four chords and more, each a fifth below the last, come about one and a half times a movement (232 runs of three falls or more in 149 movements). In some the chords are seventh chords, each seventh held over from the chord before and resolved by step: III7, VI7, ii7, V7, i in op. 3 no. 7 (bars 32 to 34). One runs the whole diatonic circle: vii, iii, vi, ii, V, I, IV (op. 1 no. 12, bars 53 to 55).
   *Works on:* harmony, texture.
   *Why:* each seventh is a note kept from the chord before, so the chain moves by suspension: one voice holds while the bass falls, and resolves as the next voice holds. **[score: op. 3 no. 7]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* take a chain of chords falling by fifths through the key; make each a seventh chord whose seventh is the note the previous chord already had, held over the bass's change and resolved down a step.
   *Where:* op. 3 no. 7, second movement, bars 32 to 34; op. 1 no. 4, third movement, bars 25 to 27; op. 1 no. 12, fourth movement, bars 53 to 55. **[score: those movements]**
3. **Suspensions.** A source speaks of his "poignant suspensions" (in the second *Adagio* of the fourth concerto); in the labelled movements 14% of the chords carry a held or delayed note in their label.
   *Works on:* harmony, melody.
   *How:* about one chord in seven, delay a note of the chord by holding the note before it, and resolve it by step.
   *Where:* op. 6 no. 4, the second *Adagio* [Wikipedia, Arcangelo Corelli]; the trio sonatas **[score: the 149 DCML movements]**.
4. **A cadence figure:** up a fourth, a long note a half step lower, a short return (C5, F5, E5, F5), far more than his contemporaries use it, in fifteen works.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* before a close, leap up a fourth, fall a half step on a long note, and step back quickly.
   *Where:* op. 6 no. 6, second movement, bar 2, and fourteen other works. **[score: the profile]**
5. **A bass that walks in quarters.** In the *Allegro* of op. 1 no. 1, 67 of the bass's 116 notes are quarters and 38 halves: a steady walk.
   *Works on:* rhythm.
   *How:* under quick music, give the bass even quarters, now and then a half note.
   *Where:* op. 1 no. 1, second movement. **[score: op. 1 no. 1]**
6. **Clarity and sobriety:** his style is praised for clarity and sober, expressive melody; chromatic notes are rare; in the profile no chord of his is a diminished seventh. Real fugues are rare too.
   *Works on:* harmony, melody.
   *How:* keep to the notes of the key, avoid the diminished seventh, and let the voices move in coordination rather than in strict fugue.
   *Where:* his music in general. [Wikipedia, Arcangelo Corelli] **[score: the profile]**
7. **The violin:** double stops explored more intensely, and bariolage (the same note sounded on two strings in turn); his players were said to make a sound uniform and powerful, like an organ's.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *How:* not a technique for this page's sonatas: a list of his violin's devices.
   *Where:* his violin music in general. [Wikipedia, Arcangelo Corelli]

### The chamber sonatas' dances (op. 4, 1694): the corrente, the giga, the gavotta

Read on 28 November 2026 for composer-blend's piece 84 (the tune made of the holes): the eight correnti and four gighe of the *Sonate da camera* op. 4 from the scores, the Gavotta of op. 4 no. 5 from the score and Christopher Wintle's analysis of Corelli's dance rhythms. The readings are composer-study's `checks/readings-for-84-2026-11-28/`.

8. **The second violin falls silent and the first goes on.** Counted at every onset of either violin in the twelve dances: the first violin alone begins a note at 49% of them, the two together at 40%, the second alone at 12%. Within its movement the second violin rests 12% of its beats, the first 3%. In two gighe the second drops out for long stretches while the first runs on: op. 4 no. 7 (65 of 160 beats) and no. 12 (45 of 112).
   *Works on:* texture.
   *How:* let the first violin carry the dance without a break, and give the second whole phrases of rest.
   *Where:* op. 4 no. 7, Giga; no. 12, Giga; no. 11, Corrente (each violin rests 26 and 28 of 218 beats). **[score: the op. 4 correnti and gighe]**
9. **The corrente's leaps by a fourth, back and forth.** In the first violin, leaps of a fourth or a fifth are 6% to 31% of its moves, dance by dance; the profile finds his correnti's figures up a fourth and down a fourth (D5, G5, D5, E5) far more often than other composers' courantes do. Where they come one after another, the line leaps up and down by a fourth or more at every note, each of its two strands moving by a step or less: 44 notes in eight such runs in the Corrente of op. 4 no. 11, 25 in one in no. 3, 41 in ten in the Giga of no. 7.
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm.
   *How:* in the corrente's quick three, let the top line swing up and down a fourth for a bar or two before it moves on by step.
   *Where:* op. 4 no. 3, Corrente, bars 9 and 16 to 17; no. 11, Corrente, bars 14 and 43; no. 1, Corrente, bar 13. **[score: the op. 4 correnti; the profile, courante (chamber)]**
10. **A dance made of units that copy one another, and an echo.** His dances are built of units of a few bars (often four), the later units taking the earlier ones as templates; a frequent way of lengthening a dance is to repeat its last unit as an echo.
    *Works on:* form.
    *How:* build each half of the dance of four-bar units; make each later unit a copy of an earlier one with small changes, and lengthen a half by repeating its last unit as an echo.
    *Where:* the gavotte of op. 2 no. 1; the gavotta of op. 4 no. 5. [Wintle]
11. **The rests filled in, unit by unit.** Over the course of the Gavotta of op. 4 no. 5 the rests are taken away. Read in the score: both violins rest for an eighth in bars 1 to 3, again in bars 5 and 6, and not once from bar 7 to the end (bar 12); the bass rests in bars 1 and 5.
    *Works on:* rhythm, form.
    *How:* begin a short dance with rests inside its figures; at each new unit, fill in more of them, until in the last unit there are none.
    *Where:* op. 4 no. 5, Gavotta, bars 1 to 12. **[score: op. 4 no. 5, Gavotta]** [Wintle]
