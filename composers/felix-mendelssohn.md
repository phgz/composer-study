# Felix Mendelssohn (1809-1847)

Begun by Claude on 22 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** One kind of piece: the scherzos. **Scores:** yes. score-reader's catalogue (version 20) holds 76 of his works, among them four scherzos: the Octet's third movement, the Scherzo in E minor for piano op. 16 no. 2, and the second movements of the quartets op. 44 no. 2 and no. 3. They were read by measurement from the ready copy; each technique marked **[score]** says where. The overture and scherzo of *A Midsummer Night's Dream* are not in the catalogue.

## Techniques

1. **One short note value from end to end.** In the two quartet scherzos, 78% and 84% of all the notes are eighths; in the Octet's the running notes are sixteenths. **[score]**
2. **Pianissimo and staccato throughout** (the Octet: *Allegro leggierissimo*, with the composer's own warning to keep it so). [Sussex]
   In the quartets soft is the ground and loud is a jab: against 80 and 118 markings of *p* and *pp* stand 51 and 88 sudden accents (*sf*), about one every four or five bars. **[score: op. 44 nos. 2 and 3]**
3. **The running line changes hands about every three bars.** 75 times in 199 bars, 102 times in 290. **[score: op. 44 nos. 2 and 3]**
4. **Repeated notes inside the running.** One quick step in seven to ten is the same note again. **[score]**
5. **It ends by vanishing.** The Octet's scherzo closes on an arpeggio rising through the chord of its keynote to the top of the violin, pianissimo and fading, "in a puff of air"; the piano scherzo ends on a rising broken chord too. The quartet scherzos end *p*. **[score; Sussex]**
6. **Once, a single violin comes forward** with trills and smoother playing, in the middle (Octet). [Sussex]
7. **Where it came from.** The Octet's scherzo was prompted, his sister said, by lines of Goethe about clouds and mist passing and the sky brightening; it leads to the *Midsummer Night's Dream* overture, written just after. [Sussex]
8. **Metres:** two (Octet), three (op. 44 no. 2), six-eight (op. 44 no. 3), four (op. 16 no. 2). Long in bars, short in time: 244 to 311 bars. **[score]**

From piece 64's search of its own lines in the 75 works that could be read: his quick even notes move by step so often that small figures are common property. A neighbour note and six steps down, in even notes, is in thirteen of the works; a turn on the leading note and back, in seven; a turn followed by eight steps up the scale, in one (the finale of the quartet op. 12).
