# Hildegard von Bingen (1098-1179)

Begun by Claude on 25 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 74b. It grows each time she is used.

**Scope so far.** Her chants, the songs of the *Symphonia*: one unaccompanied line to her own Latin words. **Scores:** yes, in modern transcription. score-reader's catalogue (version 25) holds seven of her chants, from GregoBase, the transcribers' square-note editions; all seven were read by measurement from Philip's ready-read copy. Chant has no written rhythm, so these readings give every note the same length: what is measured is the pitch, the leaps, and how many notes fall on a syllable. ## Techniques

### The chants of the *Symphonia*

1. **One line, alone.** Her music is a single melodic line, without accompaniment, set to her own poetry; the *Symphonia* gathers antiphons, hymns, sequences and responsories.
   *Works on:* texture, background.
   *How:* one voice, no harmony; the words her own.
   *Where:* the *Symphonia*. [Wikipedia, Hildegard]
2. **A wide range.** Her melodies cover up to two and a half octaves, unlike the usual chant of the choir. In the seven read, the span is an octave and a fourth in five of them (17 semitones), an octave and a seventh in *O nobilissima viriditas* (from F3 to E5).
   *Works on:* melody.
   *Why:* her melodies have been said to soar and to push past the bounds of Gregorian chant. [Wikipedia, Hildegard] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* let one line travel well over an octave, from low in the voice to high, within one song.
   *Where:* all seven; the widest *O nobilissima viriditas*. [Morent] **[score: the seven chants]**
3. **The leap of a fifth up from the keynote, at the start.** Five of the seven begin by leaping up a fifth from the note they end on: E to B (*O aeterne Deus*, *O virtus sapientiae*), D to A (*O frondens virga*, *O pastor animarum*, *O cruor sanguinis*). Across the seven, a fifth up is nearly three times as common as a fifth down, and two thirds of all leaps of a fourth or more go up.
   *Works on:* melody, harmony.
   *Why:* she uses the church modes with her own stress on fifths and octaves. [Morent] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* open a phrase on the keynote and leap straight up a fifth; climb by leaps and come down by step.
   *Where:* the openings of five of the seven. **[score: the seven chants]**
4. **Many notes to a syllable.** From under two notes a syllable (*O aeterne Deus*, 1.7) to over five (*O nobilissima viriditas*, 5.3); her music is called highly melismatic, often with melodic units that come back.
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm.
   *How:* hold a single syllable over a long run of notes, more in the songs of praise; let short figures recur within the run.
   *Where:* the seven chants. [Wikipedia, Hildegard] **[score: the seven chants]**
5. **Mostly steps between the leaps.** Two thirds of the moves from note to note are steps; one in seven is the same note again; a third or more is rare.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* move by step and repeated notes, and keep the leaps (fifths above all) for the turns of the line.
   *Where:* the seven chants together. **[score: the seven chants]**
6. **Modal ambiguity.** She prefers certain church modes, but with modal ambiguity: the line can leave doubt about which note is home.
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *How:* let the line linger on notes other than its final, so that the keynote is felt late or in doubt.
   *Where:* her chants in general. [Morent]
7. **A sung play in which only the Devil cannot sing.** The *Ordo Virtutum*, a morality play with music (82 songs), the earliest surviving musical drama not tied to a liturgy: every character sings plainchant except the Devil, whose part is spoken or shouted.
   *Works on:* form, colour, background.
   *How:* give every voice of good a sung line; give the voice of evil no music at all.
   *Where:* the *Ordo Virtutum*. [Wikipedia, Hildegard]

## What she does not do

*In the seven read.*

- Accompany the line: one voice only.
- Leap down as often as up: two thirds of her leaps rise.
