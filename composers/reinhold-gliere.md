# Reinhold Glière (1875-1956)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 68. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two kinds of piece: the epic programme symphony (the Third, *Ilya Muromets*, 1911) and the late concertos for instruments and a voice without words (harp, 1938; coloratura soprano, 1943), with a line on the ballet. **Scores:** none in score-reader's catalogue (the sheet marks him "partly" readable; the catalogue of version 25 holds nothing of his), and his works are in copyright in some countries, so nothing below was checked against notes. It rests on programme notes, a recording's booklet and encyclopaedias. Added on 24 November 2026, for piece 68b: how the coloratura concerto writes the voice, from a review and an article. Added on 25 November 2026, for piece 74: the same concerto again, from two articles not read before (techniques 18 to 20).

## Techniques

### The epic symphony: *Ilya Muromets*

1. **A brass chorale for the old hero.** The mentor-hero Svyatogor is introduced by a brass chorale marked *tranquillo misterioso*; a wide-leaping new tune follows on the second violins and the cor anglais.
   *Works on:* colour, harmony.
   *How:* give an ancient, solemn figure a slow chorale in the brass, soft and mysterious, and answer it with a wide-leaping tune on an unusual mixture (second violins and cor anglais).
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, first movement. [Chandos; ASO]

2. **An Orthodox chant for the pilgrims, first low in the winds, last as a full hymn.** The chant (an old Church Slavonic text) is heard first on cor anglais and bass clarinet in the slow introduction; in the last movement it returns as a full hymn and grows richer as the heavenly army multiplies.
   *Works on:* melody, colour, form.
   *Why:* the two old pilgrims are the chant; its growth at the end is the army of heaven multiplying. [Chandos; ASO] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* introduce a chant-like tune softly on two dark woodwinds; bring it back at the end in full harmony and grow it by adding parts as the music builds.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, the slow introduction and the last movement. [Chandos; ASO]

3. **The hero's fanfare, angular, coming back far off and then noble.** An angular fanfare motif for Ilya at the start of the first Allegro; distant fanfares in the second movement; in the finale a new victory melody joins Ilya's first theme for a climax.
   *Works on:* melody, form.
   *How:* give the hero a short angular brass call; recall it softly far off in another movement; at the end combine it with a new broad tune.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, first, second and last movements. [ASO; Chandos]

4. **The whole-tone scale for terror and the supernatural.**
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *Why:* the booklet hears it as the sign of terror and the supernatural, a line that runs back to Glinka's *Ruslan and Ludmilla*. [Chandos] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* where a threat or a magic appears, build the harmony or the line on the whole-tone scale.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, second movement (the Nightingale robber). [Chandos]

5. **A forest of birdsong, long and voluptuous.** Shuddering strings and lamenting woodwind open the Nightingale movement; his enchantment is a long, voluptuous *Andante* full of birdsong in the woodwind; strings *sul ponticello* for an icy sound.
   *Works on:* colour, texture.
   *Why:* the birdsong is the robber's enchantment, and the booklet compares the slow music to Scriabin's and the climax to the love music of *Tristan*. [Chandos; ASO] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* over trembling strings, let woodwinds play bird calls that come forward one by one; for cold, play the strings near the bridge.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, second movement. [Chandos; ASO]

6. **A melody in the baritone register, led by the cellos.**
   *Works on:* melody, colour.
   *Why:* a Russian strand that the note traces back to Glinka. [ASO] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* give a broad tune to the cellos in their middle register, low enough to sound like a baritone.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3. [ASO]

7. **Broad frescos, not nervous fragments.** Long time-spans and orchestral power taken from Wagner, the picture-making of Strauss's tone poems; the music is broad and panoramic.
   *Works on:* form.
   *Why:* the note sets it against Mahler's "nervous discontinuities": these are large "mighty frescos". [ASO] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* let each episode last, build slowly to its climax, and keep the change of scene for the big sections.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, as a whole (70 to 80 minutes uncut). [ASO; Wikipedia, Symphony 3]

8. **The end turned to stone, and a return backwards through the work.** At the last climax (*maestoso solenne*) the hero is turned to stone with a thick, ringing brass climax; then the earlier themes come back in a sad transformation of the noble tune, running back to the first movement's chorale; the strings give way to contrabassoon and tuba; the slow introduction returns, so that the life ends "in immobility" where it began.
   *Works on:* form, colour, loudness.
   *Why:* the petrification of Ilya; the note calls the coda's backward return a "cyclic regression". [Chandos; ASO] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* after the loudest climax, recall the earlier themes in reverse order, slower and darker each time, handing the line down to the lowest instruments, and close on the opening's music.
   *Where:* Symphony no. 3, the end of the last movement. [Chandos; ASO]

### The late concertos: the harp, a voice without words

9. **A voice without words as the concerto's soloist.** A coloratura soprano sings a two-movement concerto on vowels: an *Andante*, then a light, humorous *Allegro*.
   *Works on:* colour, form.
   *Why:* a whole concerto for that "instrument" was new, though a wordless voice was not (Rachmaninoff's *Vocalise*); written in the war, in an old-fashioned, nostalgic idiom meant to give listeners relief. [sin80; BBC Music Magazine] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* write for a high voice as for a solo instrument, on one vowel, with long lines and runs, over a small orchestra.
   *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, op. 82 (1943). [sin80; BBC Music Magazine]

10. **The orchestra first, unharmonised; then the winds; then the voice.** The concerto opens with a sad tune in the strings alone, without harmony; the winds fill the texture; the soprano enters soon; long lines then rise from climax to climax over a rich orchestra.
    *Works on:* texture, form.
    *How:* begin with the tune bare in the strings in unison; add the woodwind harmony; then bring in the soloist on a long line that climbs in waves.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, first movement. [sin80]

11. **A light orchestra with harp and glockenspiel.** Pairs of woodwinds, three horns, timpani, triangle, glockenspiel, harp and strings, beside the voice.
    *Works on:* colour.
    *How:* keep the orchestra classical in size, and give the high colours (harp, glockenspiel, triangle) to the moments where the voice sings highest.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano (scoring). [sin80]

12. **Classical form, Russian romantic colour.** The harp concerto joins features of the Viennese classical style with Russian romantic nationalism; three movements, in E flat, about 25 minutes; he took the harpist Ksenia Erdely's advice so fully that he offered to name her co-composer.
    *Works on:* form, background.
    *How:* not a recipe on its own: it says that the late concertos keep classical forms and fill them with romantic melody and colour, written for what the solo instrument can do.
    *Where:* Harp concerto, op. 74 (1938). [Wikipedia, Harp Concerto]

13. **Concertos for neglected instruments.** Harp, coloratura voice, then horn (1951) and cello: works meant to enrich their small repertoire.
    *Works on:* background.
    *How:* not a technique: where his late music is.
    *Where:* the concertos, op. 74, 82, 87, 91. [BBC Music Magazine; Wikipedia, Glière]

15. **The voice sings on a vowel it chooses itself, with no breath written in.** The score gives no instruction about the vowel, and no place to breathe; one soprano chose "ah".
    *Works on:* colour.
    *Why:* the whole work treats the voice as an instrument of almost limitless possibilities, and leaves the expression to the singer. [Interlude; Seen and Heard] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* write the voice's line as for an instrument, without words or breaths; let one open vowel carry it.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, op. 82. [Interlude; Seen and Heard]
16. **Long phrases, melancholy that bursts into radiance.** The first movement is warmly scored, with long phrases, a thread of melancholy that now and then opens into radiance.
    *Works on:* melody, form.
    *How:* keep the voice's phrases long; let a sad line rise, now and then, into a bright, full moment, and fall back.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, first movement, *Andante*. [Seen and Heard; Interlude]
17. **A Russian scherzo with birdsong in the voice.** The *Allegro* is a good-natured, humorous scherzo in a Russian manner; the voice has ornamented arabesques and flourishes like a nightingale's.
    *Works on:* rhythm, melody, colour.
    *How:* for a quick finale, give the voice light runs and turns, bird calls, over a dance in the orchestra.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, second movement, *Allegro*. [Seen and Heard; Interlude]

*Added on 25 November 2026, when he was drawn again for composer-blend's piece 74, before it was composed, from two articles not read before.*

18. **A line conceived for an instrument, with almost nowhere to breathe.** The score says nothing about what kind of sound the singer should make, and marks so few breaths that the writer thinks Glière imagined the voice as an instrument without limits; another writer, cited in a later article, believes the concerto was conceived instrumentally rather than vocally, since there are very few places for the singer to breathe. It is, the later article says, the only concerto among the textless vocal works of its time (it names Villa-Lobos, Copland and Szymanowski for the others).
    *Works on:* melody, form.
    *Why:* the voice set in a traditionally instrumental place, its expression left to the singer. [Buja 2018; Buja 2023] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* write the voice's phrases as long as an instrument's, joined, the gaps between them few and short; let the phrase end where the music ends it, not where a singer would breathe.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, op. 82. [Buja 2018; Buja 2023]
19. **Two movements, two qualities of the voice.** Each movement shows a different side: the *Andante* its lyricism, melancholy; the *Allegro* its brilliance, lighter.
    *Works on:* form.
    *How:* in a piece of two halves, give the voice its long sad line in the first and its quick bright runs in the second, so that the second answers the first.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, op. 82. [Buja 2023]
20. **An orchestra that sounds older than its year.** The writer finds it hard to imagine a work of 1942 that places itself further back in time; it brought him a Stalin Prize, first class, in 1946.
    *Works on:* colour, background.
    *Why:* it agrees with technique 9's reason (an old-fashioned, nostalgic idiom in the war, for relief). [Buja 2018] *(a listener)*
    *How:* keep the orchestra's harmony and doubling of the nineteenth century around the voice: sustained strings, woodwind answers, harp, no modern clash.
    *Where:* Concerto for coloratura soprano, op. 82. [Buja 2018]

### The ballet

14. **A touch of the pentatonic for a far country.** In *The Red Poppy*, set in China, five-note touches mark the Chinese characters.
    *Works on:* melody, harmony.
    *How:* colour a character's music with a few five-note turns, without building the whole on them.
    *Where:* *The Red Poppy* (1927). [BBC Music Magazine]

## Walks through whole works

**Symphony no. 3, *Ilya Muromets*** (four movements on episodes of the legend; from the notes, which give the order, not bars). I: the pilgrims' chant in the slow introduction, Svyatogor's brass chorale, his death, and Ilya's fanfare at the Allegro; the movement ends as Ilya's theme gathers richness before his ride. II: the forest of the Nightingale robber, shuddering and dark, the long voluptuous birdsong of his enchantment, Ilya's arrow; the forest left dark and threatening. III: Prince Vladimir's feast, brilliant, dancing. IV: the battle with the Tartars (fugues, "oriental cries"), the heavenly army that multiplies (the chant as a full hymn), the petrification at the climax, the backward return through the work, and the opening's music at the end. [Chandos; ASO; Bard; Wikipedia, Symphony 3]

## Numbers

- **Symphony no. 3:** 70 to 80 minutes uncut, often recorded with cuts in the last movement; quadruple woodwind, eight horns, four or five trumpets, four trombones, tuba, two harps, celesta and a large percussion section. [Wikipedia, Symphony 3; Chandos]
- **Concerto for coloratura soprano:** two movements, about 10 to 14 minutes. [sin80]
- **Harp concerto:** three movements, about 25 minutes. [Wikipedia, Harp Concerto]
