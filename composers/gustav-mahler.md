## Techniques

### The songs with orchestra of the middle years (*Kindertotenlieder*)

1. **A large orchestra used as a chamber orchestra.** The cycle is scored for a solo voice and an orchestra with piccolo, flutes, oboes, cor anglais, clarinets and bass clarinet, bassoons and contrabassoon, four horns, timpani, glockenspiel, tam-tam, celesta, harp and strings; it is used at chamber scale. In the voice and piano score the accompaniment has about half the notes of his contemporaries' songs (2.98 a quarter, against 5.73).
   *Works on:* texture, colour.
   *Why:* used at that scale, the instruments gave him a wide variety of timbres to explore. [Wikipedia, Kindertotenlieder] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* write for many kinds of instrument but let only a few play at a time, each heard as itself; keep the accompaniment thin, about three notes a beat.
   *Where:* the whole cycle. [Wikipedia, Kindertotenlieder] **[score: the profile, "song, middle"]**
2. **Two bare lines at the start.** For the first eight bars of the first song exactly two notes sound at a time: two lines in counterpoint, without chords, before the voice comes in. Afterwards the accompaniment thickens only a little (about 2.7 notes at once).
   *Works on:* texture, form.
   *How:* open with two instruments, each with its own line, and nothing else; add a third voice only when the singer enters.
   *Where:* *Nun will die Sonn' so hell aufgeh'n*, bars 1 to 8. **[score: song 1]**
3. **One keynote, and the major flickers.** The first song never changes key: D minor from the first bar to the last. Its tonic chord is minor 53 times and major 13 times, a chord or two at a time (bars 11, 15, 32, 35 to 37, 53, 62, 73, 77), and the song ends on the minor.
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *Why:* the major is never established as a key: it comes for a chord over the same keynote and is gone, and the last major chord (bar 77) is followed by the minor close. **[score: song 1]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* keep one keynote and one key for the whole song; now and then make the tonic chord major for a chord or two, more often in the middle; let the last flicker come shortly before the end, and end on the minor.
   *Where:* *Nun will die Sonn' so hell aufgeh'n*, the bars listed. **[score: song 1]**
4. **The voice leans against the chord.** More than a third of the melody's notes (36%) are outside the chord under them, against 17% in his contemporaries' songs. A third of those are struck with the chord, and most (62%) then move by a step.
   *Works on:* melody, harmony.
   *How:* put about one sung note in three outside the chord, many of them on the beat with the chord, and resolve them by step.
   *Where:* the five songs. **[score: the profile, "song, middle"]**
5. **Down a half step, the same note, down a half step.** A slow fall by half steps with a repeated note in the middle, in even notes; in his contemporaries' songs it is all but absent.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* in the voice, four even notes: a half step down, the same note again, another half step down (A flat, G, G, F sharp); then a leap up.
   *Where:* *Nun will die Sonn' so hell aufgeh'n*, bar 13 (A flat, G, G, F sharp, then up to D); *Wenn dein Mütterlein*, bar 30 (C sharp, C, C, B, then up to G). **[score: the profile, "song, middle"]**
6. **Few chords, and cadences that do not land.** 1.40 chords a bar against 2.21; of the cadences, 28% are deceptive or evaded, against 23% that are perfect.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *How:* change chord slowly, about once a bar; where a cadence is set up, swerve away from it about as often as it is completed.
   *Where:* the five songs. **[score: the profile, "song, middle"]**
7. **The second song turns to the major and back,** over its keynote: C minor, C major at bar 15, minor at 20, major at 26, and major from bar 58; but after five bars of dominant chords (68 to 72) its last chord is the minor tonic.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *Why:* the major passage that closes the song is not allowed to end it: the dominant it prepares resolves to the minor chord. **[score: song 2]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* turn the same keynote's minor to major for a passage, back, again; make the last passage major, prepare its close with the dominant for several bars, and end on the minor chord instead.
   *Where:* *Nun seh' ich wohl, warum so dunkle Flammen*: bars 15, 20, 26 and 58 (the analysts' local keys), the close at 68 to 73. **[score: song 2]**
8. **The cycle ends in the light.** The last song is in D minor, turns to the sixth degree at bar 37, back to D minor at 45, and is in D major from bar 98 to the end, the last 36 of its 133 bars. In the major part the piano's top line moves more (2.6 notes a quarter against 1.7 before), in even eighths.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *Why:* the last song ends in a major key and a mood of transcendence; the article reads the end as saying that death is powerful and love stronger. [Wikipedia, Kindertotenlieder] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* keep the keynote of the cycle's first song; spend most of the last song in its minor, and give the last quarter to the major, with a steady figure of even notes in the accompaniment.
   *Where:* *In diesem Wetter, in diesem Braus*, bars 37, 45 and 98 to the end. **[score: song 5; Wikipedia, Kindertotenlieder]**
9. **The keys of the cycle:** D minor, C minor, C minor, E flat major, D minor to D major. The five songs are meant as one unit, sung without a break.
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* a list of keys; the cycle begins and ends on D.
   *Where:* the five songs. [Wikipedia, Kindertotenlieder]
10. **The cello's tune in the last song's postlude** alludes to the first subject of the finale of his Third Symphony.
    *Works on:* background.
    *How:* not a technique: a link between two works.
    *Where:* *In diesem Wetter*, the postlude. [Wikipedia, Kindertotenlieder]

### The strophic song of the Wayfarer years (*Lieder eines fahrenden Gesellen*, 1884-85)

Added on 27 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 80: *Gieng heut morgen übers Feld*, the second song, read by measurement (OpenScore's voice and piano edition), with what sources say of it.

11. **The second verse sung as the first, the refrain's word changed.** The voice's tune at bar 30 ("Auch die Glockenblum' am Feld") is note for note and beat for beat the tune of bar 2 ("Gieng heut morgen übers Feld"); what changes is the bird's word in the refrain, "Zink! Zink!" (bars 19 and 20) becoming "Kling! Kling!" (46 and 47).
    *Works on:* form, melody.
    *How:* sing a second verse to the first verse's tune unchanged, and let the change be in a word of the refrain and what the piano does with it.
    *Where:* *Gieng heut morgen übers Feld*, bars 2 to 29 and 30 to 53. **[score: Gieng heut morgen]**
12. **The third verse: the tune's head in a far key, slower, its top held.** At bar 62 the key signature goes from two sharps to five (D major to B major); the voice's third verse ("Und da fing im Sonnenschein", bar 65) begins with the same intervals as the first (a fourth down, a fourth up, three steps up) and holds the top note, F sharp 5, for ten beats; the tempo words are "Allmählig in ein sehr gemächliches Tempo einlenken" (bar 58) and "Noch etwas langsamer" (bar 64).
    *Works on:* melody, harmony, form.
    *How:* for the last verse move the tune's opening to a key far from the first, slow down, and stretch its highest note far beyond its first length.
    *Where:* *Gieng heut morgen übers Feld*, bars 58 to 75. **[score: Gieng heut morgen]**
13. **The happy song ends turned.** From bar 103, "Sehr leise und langsam", the voice takes the tune's head once more a fifth lower (F sharp 4, C sharp 4, F sharp 4, then up by step) on "Nun fängt auch mein Glück wohl an?!", and the song closes on "mir nimmer, nimmer blühen kann" (bars 117 to 127). It "begins in D major and ends in F♯ major" [Wikipedia, Lieder eines fahrenden Gesellen]; it is "the only song of the four in a major key" [Nashville Symphony].
    *Works on:* form, melody, background.
    *How:* after the verses, bring the tune's first notes back very soft and slow, in another key, under words that turn the song's joy into its opposite.
    *Where:* *Gieng heut morgen übers Feld*, bars 103 to 127. **[score: Gieng heut morgen; Wikipedia]**
14. **The song's tune becomes the symphony's.** "The melody of this movement, as well as much of the orchestration, was later reused by Mahler and developed into the 'A' theme of the first movement of his Symphony No. 1" [Wikipedia, Lieder eines fahrenden Gesellen]; there it is "a bright exposition in contrast with the slower and darker introduction" [Mahler Foundation, Symphony No. 1].
    *Works on:* background, form.
    *How:* not a technique on its own: the same tune, taken from a song, is the material of a symphony's first movement.
    *Where:* the First Symphony, first movement. Not read here. [Wikipedia; Mahler Foundation]

## Walks through whole works

***Nun will die Sonn' so hell aufgeh'n*** (84 bars, D minor throughout). Two bare lines for eight bars; the voice enters, the accompaniment never much more than three notes at once. The major tonic chord comes and goes eleven times (from bar 11 to bar 77); the song ends in the minor (bar 82). The voice sings about half the time (48%), between D4 and E flat 5.

***In diesem Wetter, in diesem Braus*** (133 bars). D minor; the sixth degree at bar 37; D minor again at 45; D major from bar 98, with the piano in even eighths, to the end.

## What he does not do

*In the first song.*

- Change key: the major comes only as a chord over the same keynote.
- Thicken the accompaniment: it stays between two and three notes at once.

*In the cycle:* let a song in the minor end in the major before the last one. The first ends on its minor chord, the second on its minor chord after a major passage, the third on the dominant; the fourth is in E flat major throughout.
