## Techniques

### The ballad (*Der Erlkönig*, 1842)

1. **The ride is a scale.** Under a chord held in the right hand, the left hand runs up an octave and down again in sixteenths, from D2 to D3 and back, bar after bar: two thirds of the notes of the first eight bars are sixteenths, all of them in bars 13 to 20. *Geschwind*, 4/4, D minor.
   *Works on:* rhythm, texture.
   *How:* hold the chord of the key in the right hand; in the left hand, run up the scale of the key through an octave and back down in sixteenths, without a break, under the voice.
   *Where:* *Der Erlkönig*, bars 1 to 8 and 13 to 20, and again in the storm. **[score: Der Erlkönig]**
2. **The lure stops the ride.** Where the Elf King sings (bars 37 to 48, 61 to 72, 85 to 92), the score asks for a voice *rather whispering and luring*; the running sixteenths stop altogether, and the left hand plays broken chords, partly in triplets, under high chords in the right hand, in the major (F major, B flat).
   *Works on:* texture, harmony, colour.
   *Why:* the running bass stops entirely only under the Elf King, the child's answers to him, and the end; everywhere else the ride goes on under the voice. **[score: Der Erlkönig]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* for the tempting voice, stop the running bass; give the left hand gentle broken chords (triplets among them) and the right hand soft high chords, in the relative major.
   *Where:* *Der Erlkönig*, bar 37 (the marking) to 48; 61 to 72; 85 to 92. **[score: Der Erlkönig]**
3. **The child's cry, three times, each higher.** "Mein Vater, mein Vater" on repeated notes: the first time B flat, B flat, B flat, A, A, A, A flat (from B flat 4, bar 46); the second the same shape a fourth higher (from E flat 5, bar 70); the third from E5, rising instead of falling, E, E, F, F sharp, F sharp, F sharp, G, G, G, A flat (bars 94 to 96).
   *Works on:* melody, form.
   *Why:* the cry keeps its shape and its repeated notes, so that only its height, and at the end its direction, changes. **[score: Der Erlkönig]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* a cry of three repeated notes, then three a half step lower, then the next half step down; the second time the same, a fourth higher; the third time start higher still and climb by half steps instead.
   *Where:* *Der Erlkönig*, bars 46 to 49, 70 to 73, 94 to 97. **[score: Der Erlkönig]**
4. **Repeated notes, then a half step.** Three notes the same, the last a half step higher (D4, D4, D4, E flat 4 at the first question): a figure her songs use far more than her contemporaries'.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* in the voice, three notes on one pitch, then a half step up (or, in the cry, down).
   *Where:* *Der Erlkönig*, bar 5; *O lass mich dein gedenken*, bar 19; *Wenn der Abendstern die Rosen*, bar 21. **[score: the profile, "song, all"]**
5. **At the threat, both hands run.** At "so brauch' ich Gewalt" (by force) the only bars of the song where both hands are in sixteenths together, four notes a quarter each (bars 93 to 96); a *rallentando* at 99, *a tempo* at 101.
   *Works on:* texture, loudness, form.
   *How:* at the height of the danger, put both hands into running sixteenths at once, for a few bars only; then hold back, and go on.
   *Where:* *Der Erlkönig*, bars 93 to 101. **[score: Der Erlkönig]**
6. **The end slows and falls an octave.** The running stops from bar 109; the last line (bars 116 to 119) is marked *Andante*, and on "war tot" (was dead) the voice falls from D5 to D4; the piano closes with chords over a low bass.
   *Works on:* form, melody.
   *How:* stop the ride some bars before the end; set the last line slower; let the voice fall an octave on its last word; close with plain chords in the piano.
   *Where:* *Der Erlkönig*, bars 109 to 122. **[score: Der Erlkönig]**
7. **Through-composed and lyrical,** her songs recall the Mendelssohns; her song-writing was learned from Loewe; the *Erlkönig* is among her earliest settings, all of Goethe. She wrote few solo songs.
   *Works on:* background, form.
   *How:* not a technique: where her songs stand.
   *Where:* [Loges]
8. **Her harmony, in general:** sudden shifts of key; many seventh chords, the diminished seventh used to reach many different resolutions; a key set up by its dominant seventh; rhythms in several layers at once.
   *Works on:* harmony, rhythm.
   *How:* set up each new key with its dominant seventh; move by surprise to another; use the diminished seventh as a pivot that can go anywhere; let several rhythmic layers sound together.
   *Where:* her music in general (the source names no work). [Wikipedia, Emilie Mayer]

## Walks through whole works

***Der Erlkönig*** (122 bars, D minor, *Geschwind*): the ride in the bass (bars 1 to 36), the father and the son; the Elf King, *rather whispering and luring*, the ride stopped (37 to 48); the first cry (46); the father calming him, the ride again (53 to 60); the Elf King (61 to 72); the second cry (70); the father (77 to 84); the Elf King (85 to 92) and his threat, both hands running (93 to 96); the third cry (94); *rallentando* (99), *a tempo* (101); the ride stops (109); the last line *Andante* (116), the voice falling an octave (118 to 119); the piano's close (119 to 122).

## What she does not do

*In the Erlkönig.*

- Let the ride run under the Elf King.
- Repeat the cry at the same height.
