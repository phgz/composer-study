## Techniques

### The partsongs for men's voices

1. **A gallop on one repeated note, then a call on the notes of the chord.** In *Lützows wilde Jagd* the first tenors sing B flat four times in eighths, then break the E flat chord (G, B flat; then E flat, B flat, E flat), the second tenors a third below them; the lowest bass stays on the keynote.
   *Works on:* rhythm, melody, texture.
   *How:* in a quick six-eight, repeat one note in even eighths, then leap about the notes of the chord, with a second voice a third under the first.
   *Where:* *Lützows wilde Jagd*, bars 2 to 4. **[score: Lützows wilde Jagd]**

2. **Repeated notes as the rule.** Against 96 partsongs by 7 other composers: repeated notes 30% of his moves against 13%; moves by step 41% against 58%; a leap followed by a step back 14% against 39%. His commonest figure is one note four times, short-long-short, 14.8 times in 1,000 figures against 0.9, in four partsongs.
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm.
   *How:* let the melody stand on one note and repeat it, leap rather than step, and do not soften a leap with a step back.
   *Where:* *Schwertlied*, bar 10; *Lützows wilde Jagd*, bar 23; *Grablied*, bar 11. **[score: the profile]**

3. **A dotted repeated note before the cadence.** A dotted eighth, a sixteenth and an eighth on one note leads into the cadence: C sharp in the first tenor of *Lützows wilde Jagd*, bar 8, and in the *Reiterlied*, bar 8.
   *Works on:* rhythm.
   *How:* just before a phrase lands, repeat one note in a dotted rhythm.
   *Where:* *Lützows wilde Jagd*, bar 8; *Reiterlied*, bar 8. **[score: Lützows wilde Jagd, Reiterlied]**

7. **Four men's voices moving together.** In eight of the eleven four-part partsongs and choruses in the catalogue, all four parts begin a note together at 71% to 97% of the moments where any begins (the *Reiterlied* 97%, *Gebet vor der Schlacht* 92%, *Schwertlied* 91%, *Lützows wilde Jagd* 81%); the *Schlummerlied* least (32%). They are a collection of four-part settings for men's chorus, op. 42 [Wikipedia, Lyre and Sword].
   *Works on:* texture.
   *How:* write four low voices in one rhythm, chord by chord, the melody on top.
   *Where:* the partsongs named. **[score: the partsongs]**

8. **The prayer: the bass on one note, a voice inside moving by half steps.** In *Gebet vor der Schlacht* the lowest bass repeats D flat, then A flat, for bars at a time, while the second bass moves F, F sharp and back under the tenors' chord.
   *Works on:* harmony, texture.
   *How:* hold the bass on the keynote and let one inner voice move a half step up and back.
   *Where:* *Gebet vor der Schlacht*, bars 2 to 8. **[score: Gebet vor der Schlacht]**

### *Der Freischütz*, from sources

4. **A Bohemian village band, and a dance that leaves one man alone.** The opera is set in Bohemia shortly after the Thirty Years' War. In its first act a peasants' march is played by an onstage band of Bohemian mountaineers; later the peasants waltz into the inn and leave Max alone on stage, in a monologue that moves freely between aria and recitative.
   *Works on:* colour, form.
   *How:* let a band be heard as a band inside the music, and let a dance go on, or go away, while one character is left with his own music.
   *Where:* Act 1. [Wikipedia, Der Freischütz; Operascribe]

5. **The horn and the clarinet as colours.** A critic of 1904, quoted in a modern account of the opera, praises his keen sense of the colours of the oboe and the clarinet, and finds in his horn writing a new means of picturesque, romantic expression.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *Why:* the horn for picturesque, romantic expression. [Operascribe, quoting R. Aldrich] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* give the clarinet and the horns the music's colour, not only its harmony.
   *Where:* *Der Freischütz* (the source names no passage). [Operascribe]

6. **Not folk song.** That his tunes were simply German folk music is called a common misconception.
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* not a technique on its own: it warns that a tune in his manner is his, not borrowed from the people.
   *Where:* *Der Freischütz*. [Wikipedia, Der Freischütz]
