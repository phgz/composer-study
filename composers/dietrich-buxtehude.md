# Dieterich Buxtehude (about 1637-1707)

Begun by Claude on 24 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 73. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two kinds of piece: the vocal concerto on a ground, as his *Laudate pueri Dominum* (BuxWV 69) shows it; and the free organ piece in sections, as the Toccata BuxWV 155 shows it. **Scores:** yes. score-reader's catalogue (version 25) holds seventeen readings of his works (cantatas, *Membra Jesu nostri*, chorale settings, the Toccata and the *Laudate pueri*). The two above were read by measurement from Philip's ready-read copy; each technique marked **[score]** names the bars. His organ chaconnes and passacaglia, the best known of his works on a ground, are not in the catalogue.

**Measured profile:** none in `profiles/` yet.

## Techniques

### The ground (*Laudate pueri Dominum*, BuxWV 69)

1. **A ground that never changes.** An eight-bar bass in 4/4, in D, from D2 to B flat 3, stated twelve times from bar 1 to bar 96 and closed by one more bar: at every quarter of every statement the bass's lowest note is the same as in the first, and it is never moved to another key.
   *Works on:* form, harmony.
   *Why:* nothing in the bass changes, so everything that makes the piece go somewhere happens above it. **[score: Laudate pueri Dominum]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* write an eight-bar bass; play it, unchanged and in the same key, under the whole piece; build the piece from what goes on over it.
   *Where:* *Laudate pueri Dominum*, bars 1 to 97. **[score: Laudate pueri Dominum]**
2. **Each statement filled differently.** Over the same ground the number of notes changes from one statement to the next, from about 160 to about 420: the instruments play the first statement alone (320 notes); the voices enter at the second, over a thinned accompaniment (167); full again at the third to fifth; thin at the sixth (162); the fullest at the seventh (423). The two voices stop at bar 89, so the last statement is the instruments' again, as the first was.
   *Works on:* texture, form.
   *How:* over the unchanging ground, make each statement a new texture: one full, the next thin; let a statement thin out to bring in a new voice; frame the piece with a statement for the instruments alone at each end.
   *Where:* *Laudate pueri Dominum*, the twelve statements (bars 1 to 96). **[score: Laudate pueri Dominum]**
3. **The voices breathe with the ground's halves.** Of the voices' 31 phrase endings (rests of a half note or more), 11 fall in the ground's first bar, where its last cadence lands on the start of the next statement, and 8 in its fifth, its middle; the rest are scattered, so some phrases run over the join.
   *Works on:* melody, form.
   *How:* end the tune's phrases mostly on the first bar of a new statement and at the ground's midpoint, four bars in; let a few run across the join so that the ground's frame is not always heard.
   *Where:* *Laudate pueri Dominum*, bars 9 to 89. **[score: Laudate pueri Dominum]**

### The free organ piece (Toccata, BuxWV 155)

4. **Free and strict in turn.** The Toccata in D minor (140 bars) is in three sections: bars 1 to 62 free, in 4/4, in quick notes (eighths and sixteenths the most common) over pedal notes held for a bar or more (bars 10, 12, 17, 19, 24, 46, 57, 61, 62); bars 63 to 136 strict, in 3/4, imitative: a subject in the left hand at bar 63, which the pedal takes up a fourth lower at bar 67; bars 137 to 140 a close. His praeludia are described as sectional pieces that alternate free improvisation with strict counterpoint, the strict parts fugues or in a fugal manner, all with much use of the pedal.
   *Works on:* form, texture.
   *How:* alternate a free section (runs and figures over long held bass notes) with a strict one (a subject taken up by one voice after another, the pedal among them), and end with a short close.
   *Where:* Toccata BuxWV 155, bars 1 to 62, 63 to 136, 137 to 140. **[score: Toccata BuxWV 155]**; the praeludia in general [Wikipedia]
5. **The close over a held keynote.** The last four bars hold D2 in the pedal for all four while the hands play in triplets above it.
   *Works on:* form, rhythm.
   *How:* end on the keynote held in the bass for several bars, with quick figures above it in a new rhythm (triplets here).
   *Where:* Toccata BuxWV 155, bars 137 to 140. **[score: Toccata BuxWV 155]**

### In general

6. **The organ works on a ground,** two chaconnes and a passacaglia, are described as having many connected sections, many suspensions, changing metres, and even real changes of key; they are among his best-known works and influenced many later composers.
   *Works on:* form, harmony.
   *How:* over a repeating bass, make many short sections that run into one another; hold notes over into the next chord (suspensions); change the metre; move the ground into another key now and then.
   *Where:* the chaconnes BuxWV 159 and 160 and the passacaglia, by the source (no bars named). [Wikipedia, Dieterich Buxtehude]
7. **Where he stands:** organist of the Marienkirche in Lübeck from 1668; in 1673 he reorganised the evening concerts there (the Abendmusik); most of his music is vocal, more than a hundred pieces; Bach, at twenty, walked from Arnstadt to Lübeck in 1705 and stayed nearly three months, and Buxtehude is called one of his influences.
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* not a technique: where his music stands.
   *Where:* [Wikipedia, Dieterich Buxtehude]

## Walks through whole works

***Laudate pueri Dominum*** (BuxWV 69; 97 bars, 4/4, D): the ground and the instruments alone (bars 1 to 8); the two voices enter, the instruments thinned (9 to 16); fuller, statement by statement (17 to 40); thin again (41 to 48); the fullest statement (49 to 56); four more, the voices on the psalm's verses (57 to 88); the voices stop at 89 and the instruments play the last statement (89 to 96); one closing bar (97).

**Toccata BuxWV 155** (140 bars, D minor): free, over held pedal notes (bars 1 to 62); strict, in 3/4, the subject in the left hand (63) and then in the pedal (67) (63 to 136); the close over a held D (137 to 140).

## What he does not do

*In the Laudate pueri.*

- Change the ground or move it to another key: twelve statements the same. (His organ works on a ground do move it, by the source; technique 6.)

## Too well known to borrow

- His organ works on a ground, the **two chaconnes (BuxWV 159 and 160) and the passacaglia**, among his best-known works; the Praeludium **BuxWV 146** in F sharp minor, called well known; the **Prelude and Chaconne in C major, BuxWV 137**, called famous. [Wikipedia, Dieterich Buxtehude]
