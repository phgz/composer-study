# Piotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840-1893)

## Techniques

1. **Written to the ballet-master's plan.** Petipa gave him detailed instructions for each number, down to the tempo and the number of bars. A note of his own for one entry reads: sixteen bars, rococo, minuet tempo.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* fix the bar counts, the speed and the character of a dance before writing it, and write to them.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, every number; his note for one entry (sixteen bars, rococo, minuet tempo). [Wikipedia, The Nutcracker; Tchaikovsky Research]
2. **A new keyboard for one fairy.** He found the celesta in Paris in 1891, had his publisher order one, and asked that it be kept quiet so that no other Russian composer would use it first. He called its sound "divinely unusual" and placed it between a tiny piano and a glockenspiel. Petipa wanted the Sugar Plum Fairy's music to sound like drops of water shooting from a fountain.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *How:* keep one new colour for one character and nowhere else; for drops of water, short, high, separate notes on a keyboard of bells, softly.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, the Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy (the celesta; a piano may replace it, and it needs a good pianist). [Tchaikovsky Research; Simple English Wikipedia]
3. **The character dances are tiny.** Tea is 32 bars; the Reed-Flutes 77; the Trepak 84; the Sugar Plum Fairy's variation 84 (*Andante ma non troppo*, then *Presto*); Coffee 102.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* make each character dance short, 32 to about 100 bars, with one idea and one colour, and stop as soon as it is said.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, Act II: Tea, the Reed-Flutes, the Trepak, the Sugar Plum Fairy, Coffee. [Tchaikovsky Research]
4. **A tune that is only a scale.** The adagio of the pas de deux is built on a one-octave scale, embellished, sung by the cellos.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* take a plain scale of one octave as the whole tune; give it to the cellos, singing, and dress it with harmony and ornament.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, the adagio of the pas de deux; *The Seasons*, the Barcarolle, climbing a scale the same way. [Wikipedia; New York City Ballet]
5. **Children's instruments.** For the party scene he asked for the toy instruments of Haydn's and Romberg's children's symphonies: a cuckoo (two notes, G and E), a rattle, drums, cymbals.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *How:* where children play, add toy sounds (a two-note cuckoo, a rattle, small drums) to the orchestra.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, Act I, the party scene. [Tchaikovsky Research]
6. **One colour to a dance.** Coffee: strings with bassoons and English horn, an oboe solo, cymbals, fading straight into Tea.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *How:* give each dance its own small band and keep it for the whole dance; let one dance fade straight into the next.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, Coffee into Tea. [New York City Ballet]
7. **Wordless voices.** Twelve sopranos and twelve altos in the Waltz of the Snowflakes.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *How:* add high wordless voices to a dance for a cold, airy colour.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, the Waltz of the Snowflakes. [Tchaikovsky Research]
8. **A scene that grows to one chord.** As the tree grows the melody develops, and it reaches a great A major chord when the lights come up.
   *Works on:* form, loudness.
   *How:* let a tune grow louder and fuller over a long span toward one big, plain major chord, timed to the moment it stands for.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*, Act I, the growing of the tree. [New York City Ballet]
9. **He moved his own numbers about.** A tarantella from the first act became a variation in the second, half a tone lower; the Spanish and Chinese dances of the first act became Chocolate and Tea.
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* not a technique for one piece: a number can be moved, renamed and set half a tone lower where the plan needs it.
   *Where:* *The Nutcracker*: Chocolate and Tea, and the variation that was a tarantella. [Tchaikovsky Research]

## Numbers

Bars, from the score as Tchaikovsky Research gives them: Overture 182; March 88; Tea 32; Coffee 102; Chocolate 178; Trepak 84; Reed-Flutes 77; Waltz of the Flowers 353; pas de deux adagio 74; Sugar Plum Fairy 84; Waltz of the Snowflakes 407. The whole ballet lasts about 90 minutes. Keys in the concert suite: Arabian dance G minor, Chinese dance B flat, Trepak G, Reed-Flutes D, Sugar Plum Fairy E minor.

## Too well known to borrow

- **Five melodies called famous:** the Dance of the Little Swans (*Swan Lake*); the Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy and the Waltz of the Flowers (*The Nutcracker*); the Polonaise from *Eugene Onegin*; the first piano concerto. Technique 2 gives the celesta to one character: the colour can be used, the Sugar Plum Fairy's tune not. [gw2ru]
