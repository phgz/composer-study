## Techniques

1. **Written to the ballet-master's plan.** Petipa gave him detailed instructions for each number, down to the tempo and the number of bars. A note of his own for one entry reads: sixteen bars, rococo, minuet tempo. [Wikipedia, The Nutcracker; Tchaikovsky Research]
   *How:* fix the bar counts of a dance before writing it, and write to them.
2. **A new keyboard for one fairy.** He found the celesta in Paris in 1891, had his publisher order one, and asked that it be kept quiet so that no other Russian composer would use it first. He called its sound "divinely unusual" and placed it between a tiny piano and a glockenspiel. Petipa wanted the Sugar Plum Fairy's music to sound like drops of water shooting from a fountain. The part needs a good pianist; a piano may replace it. [Tchaikovsky Research; Simple English Wikipedia]
3. **The character dances are tiny.** Tea is 32 bars; the Reed-Flutes 77; the Trepak 84; the Sugar Plum Fairy's variation 84 (*Andante ma non troppo*, then *Presto*); Coffee 102. [Tchaikovsky Research]
4. **A tune that is only a scale.** The adagio of the pas de deux is built on a one-octave scale, embellished, sung by the cellos. The Barcarolle of *The Seasons* climbs a scale the same way. [Wikipedia; New York City Ballet]
5. **Children's instruments.** For the party scene he asked for the toy instruments of Haydn's and Romberg's children's symphonies: a cuckoo (two notes, G and E), a rattle, drums, cymbals. [Tchaikovsky Research]
6. **One colour to a dance.** Coffee: strings with bassoons and English horn, an oboe solo, cymbals, fading straight into Tea. [New York City Ballet]
7. **Wordless voices.** Twelve sopranos and twelve altos in the Waltz of the Snowflakes. [Tchaikovsky Research]
8. **A scene that grows to one chord.** As the tree grows the melody develops, and it reaches a great A major chord when the lights come up. [New York City Ballet]
9. **He moved his own numbers about.** A tarantella from the first act became a variation in the second, half a tone lower; the Spanish and Chinese dances of the first act became Chocolate and Tea. [Tchaikovsky Research]

## Numbers

Bars, from the score as Tchaikovsky Research gives them: Overture 182; March 88; Tea 32; Coffee 102; Chocolate 178; Trepak 84; Reed-Flutes 77; Waltz of the Flowers 353; pas de deux adagio 74; Sugar Plum Fairy 84; Waltz of the Snowflakes 407. The whole ballet lasts about 90 minutes. Keys in the concert suite: Arabian dance G minor, Chinese dance B flat, Trepak G, Reed-Flutes D, Sugar Plum Fairy E minor.
