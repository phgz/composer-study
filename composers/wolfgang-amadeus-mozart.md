# Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756-1791)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two kinds of piece: slow music in which a wind sings, and his serenades for winds; and his variations for piano on a tune of the day (added on 24 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 72: five sets read by measurement from Philip's ready-read copy, catalogue version 25, in the TAVERN edition with its chord labels). **Scores:** yes. score-reader's catalogue (version 20) holds 268 files of his: the piano sonatas, the quartets, the clarinet, bassoon and horn concertos, the clarinet and horn quintets, *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*, a divertimento for three winds. Two were read by measurement: the Adagio of the Clarinet Concerto K. 622 and the five movements of the wind divertimento K. 439b; each technique marked **[score]** says where. The *Gran Partita* K. 361 is not in the catalogue and is known here from a source.

## Techniques

1. **The soloist has eight bars, softly; then everyone says them again.** In the concerto's Adagio the winds of the orchestra are silent for bars 1 to 8, play 9 to 16, are silent 17 to 24, and play 25 to 32: the tune twice, each half said by one and then by all.
   *Works on:* texture, form.
   *How:* write a tune of eight bars for one wind, softly, over the strings; then repeat the same eight bars with the orchestra's winds joining; do the same with the next eight.
   *Where:* Clarinet Concerto K. 622, second movement, bars 1 to 32. **[score: K. 622, second movement]**
2. **Under the singer, even eighths and a bass in quarters.** In those first eight bars the two violin parts play six eighths to the bar, every bar, in broken chords (four in ten of their moves are leaps wider than a fourth); the bass has quarter notes.
   *Works on:* rhythm, texture.
   *How:* under a slow solo in 3/4, give two inner instruments unbroken eighths in broken chords (wide leaps are fine) and the bass plain quarter notes, one to a beat.
   *Where:* K. 622, second movement, bars 1 to 8. **[score: K. 622]**
3. **Three slow beats.** The Adagio is in 3/4, 98 bars.
   *Works on:* rhythm.
   *How:* for a singing slow movement, three slow beats to the bar, about a hundred bars.
   *Where:* K. 622, second movement. **[score: K. 622]**
4. **The orchestra's winds come for the repeats and the joins only:** bars 9-16, 25-32, 53-58, 74-82 and the close. Elsewhere the singer has the strings alone.
   *Works on:* form, colour.
   *Why:* the soloist sings over the strings alone, so the winds' colour marks only the repeats and the joins. **[score: K. 622]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* keep the orchestra's winds silent while the soloist sings; bring them in for the tutti repeats, for the passages that join one section to the next, and for the close.
   *Where:* K. 622, second movement, bars 9 to 16, 25 to 32, 53 to 58, 74 to 82 and 95 to 96. **[score: K. 622]**
5. **A serenade is many short movements, with a slow one in the middle of dances.** The divertimento: a quick movement (36 bars), a minuet (75), an Adagio (37), a second minuet (66), a rondo in two (157). The *Gran Partita* has seven, with two minuets, an Adagio and a Romanze.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* for a piece of several movements for winds: a quick opening, a minuet, a slow movement in the middle, a second minuet, a quick rondo; each short.
   *Where:* Divertimento K. 439b; the *Gran Partita*. **[score: K. 439b; Wikipedia]**
6. **The upper two go together in thirds and sixths.** In the divertimento's Adagio the two upper parts begin a note together 133 times, and 114 of those are a third or a sixth apart; the lowest part moves in eighths and sixteenths under them.
   *Works on:* harmony, texture.
   *How:* move two high instruments together, note against note, a third or a sixth apart nearly always; give the lowest instrument quicker notes under them.
   *Where:* Divertimento K. 439b, third movement (Adagio). **[score: K. 439b, third movement]**
7. **A band of winds in pairs** (the *Gran Partita*): two oboes, two clarinets, two basset horns, two bassoons, four horns and a double bass, which a contrabassoon sometimes replaces.
   *Works on:* colour.
   *Why:* an unusual combination for its time: a band chosen for its texture. [Wikipedia] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* a wind band in pairs, oboes, clarinets, basset horns (or a second pair of clarinets), bassoons and horns, with a double bass under them.
   *Where:* Serenade no. 10, the *Gran Partita*. [Wikipedia]
8. **A syncopated pulse under solo lines** (the *Gran Partita*'s Adagio, in E flat): the pulse runs through most of the movement; the solo lines go from oboe to clarinet to basset horn; the oboe enters first. Called operatic in feeling.
   *Works on:* rhythm, texture.
   *Why:* it makes the movement almost an operatic ensemble, passionate and sensuous (Goodwin, as quoted). [Wikipedia] *(a listener)*
   *How:* under a slow movement, a pulse of repeated notes off the beat that runs nearly throughout; over it, long solo lines handed from oboe to clarinet to a lower reed, the oboe first.
   *Where:* *Gran Partita*, the Adagio. [Wikipedia]
9. **Trios thin the band:** the first minuet's first trio is for clarinets and basset horns only; its second, in the minor, for oboe, basset horn and bassoon.
   *Works on:* texture, colour.
   *How:* in a minuet's trio, drop to a few instruments of one family or a mixed three, for a lighter and different colour; make one trio minor.
   *Where:* *Gran Partita*, the first minuet's two trios. [Wikipedia]
10. **A slow frame around a quicker minor middle** (the Romanze): Adagio, an Allegretto in C minor over a steady pulse in the bassoons, Adagio.
    *Works on:* rhythm, form.
    *How:* slow, then a quicker middle in the minor over a steady pulse in the low instruments, then slow again.
    *Where:* *Gran Partita*, the Romanze. [Wikipedia]

### Variations for piano on a tune of the day

His sets in the catalogue take their tunes from others: an arietta from Gluck's *Die Pilgrime von Mekka* (K. 455), an aria from Paisiello's *I filosofi immaginarii* (K. 398), the French song *La belle Françoise* (K. 353), a minuet by Duport (K. 573), a song from a Singspiel, *Ein Weib ist das herrlichste Ding* (K. 613). Each file prints its repeats as written, so its bars are the printed ones.

11. **Varied in front of company.** At his own concert in the Burgtheater on 23 March 1783, with the emperor present, he played variations on the Paisiello aria and, in his words to his father, "varied the aria *Unser dummer Pöbel meint*" from Gluck's opera. The Gluck variations are K. 455; the Paisiello ones probably those published as K. 398.
    *Works on:* background, form.
    *How:* not a technique: where the form was played, a tune the audience knew, varied at the keyboard.
    *Where:* the concert of 23 March 1783. [KCL, Mozart's concert at the Burgtheater, quoting his letter of 29 March 1783]
12. **A short, plain theme.** Gluck's arietta, as K. 455 gives it, is twelve bars of 2/2, five to ten notes a bar in the right hand.
    *Works on:* form, melody.
    *How:* take a tune of about twelve to sixteen bars, simple in rhythm, and state it plainly first.
    *Where:* K. 455, bars 1 to 12. **[score: K. 455]**
13. **One figure a variation, and the running moves from hand to hand.** In K. 455 the first variation runs in sixteenths in the right hand (sixteen notes a bar), the second gives the running to the left hand (sixteen a bar there, the right hand back to the tune's pace), the third is in triplets (twelve a bar).
    *Works on:* texture, rhythm.
    *How:* give each variation one figure throughout: first quick even notes over the tune in the right hand; then the same in the left hand under it; then triplets.
    *Where:* K. 455, bars 13 to 24, 25 to 36, 37 to 48. **[score: K. 455]**
14. **One variation in the minor, past the middle.** K. 455 at bar 73 of 344 (G minor, the fifth variation); K. 398 at bar 89 of 180; K. 353 at 109 of 183; K. 573 at 145 of 278; K. 613 at 290 of 495. Each goes back to the major after it.
    *Works on:* harmony, form.
    *How:* put one variation in the minor of the same keynote, a little after the middle of the set, and return to the major for the next.
    *Where:* the five sets, at the bars named (the key signatures). **[score: K. 455, K. 398, K. 353, K. 573, K. 613]**
15. **A free cadenza, then a slow variation thick with ornament.** In K. 455 a bar of 7/4 (bar 163), a free passage of 24 notes, leads into a variation whose right hand has 20 to 31 notes a bar (bars 164 to 211): runs and turns in the smallest notes around the tune. Free bars of this kind come in every set (K. 398 at 115, 139 and 140, and 156 to 161).
    *Works on:* form, melody, rhythm.
    *How:* stop the beat for one free bar of running notes; then a slow variation in which the tune is ornamented with runs and turns in very short notes.
    *Where:* K. 455, bars 163 to 211; K. 398, the bars named. **[score: K. 455, K. 398]**
16. **The last variation in another metre, with its own cadenza; then the theme's metre again.** K. 455 goes into 3/8 at bar 212, stops for free bars at 263 to 268, goes on in 3/8, and returns to 2/2 for its last 21 bars; K. 573 and K. 613 end their sets in 2/4, then return to 3/4.
    *Works on:* form, rhythm.
    *How:* make the last variation quick and in a new metre; break it once with a free passage; end in the theme's own metre, with the theme or its close.
    *Where:* K. 455, bars 212 to 344; K. 573, bars 217 to 278; K. 613, bars 364 to 495. **[score: K. 455, K. 573, K. 613]**

## Walks through whole works

**K. 455, ten variations on Gluck's *Unser dummer Pöbel meint*** (344 printed bars, G major, 2/2), by what the score shows: the theme (bars 1 to 12); sixteenths in the right hand (13 to 24); in the left (25 to 36); triplets (37 to 48); the minor (73 to 84); a free bar (163) and the slow ornamented variation (164 to 211); the last variation in 3/8 (212 to 323), with free bars at 263 to 268; 2/2 again to the end (324 to 344).

**Clarinet Concerto K. 622, second movement, Adagio** (98 bars, 3/4). From the score's parts, by where the orchestra's winds play; the solo line itself was not followed (see "Not checked").
- **Bars 1 to 32:** the tune twice, each half said first by the soloist over the strings and then by everyone (bars 9 to 16 and 25 to 32 with the winds) (technique 1). Under the soloist the violins play even eighths in broken chords and the bass quarter notes (technique 2).
- **Bars 33 to 98:** the soloist and the strings, with the winds only at bars 53 to 58, 74 to 82 and the close (technique 4).

## Too well known to borrow

- ***Eine kleine Nachtmusik*** (Serenade K. 525): one of his most famous works; one writer thinks it his most popular. Its first movement opens on a theme that climbs fast up through the chord (called a "Mannheim rocket"). [Wikipedia, Eine kleine Nachtmusik]

## Not checked

- The tempo markings of the variations (the slow one's *Adagio*, the last one's speed): not in the reading; what is slow and quick above is from the note values and the metres.
- Which variation of K. 455 begins where, beyond those named: the boundaries were read from the figures, every twelve bars, not from labels.

- The solo clarinet's own line in the Adagio: the file read does not hold it apart from the violins, so its rhythm and ornaments were not measured.
- Whether the Adagio pauses for the soloist to lead back into the tune: the file has no pause marks.
- The metre of the *Gran Partita*'s Adagio: the source as read says three; Claude believes it is in four.
- That he wrote a serenade sung to a mandolin in *Don Giovanni*: believed from memory.
