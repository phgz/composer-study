# Antonín Dvořák (1841-1904)

Begun by Claude on 22 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two kinds of piece that he often joins: the dumka and the furiant. **Scores:** yes. score-reader's catalogue (version 20) holds 61 of his works: the seventh and ninth symphonies, six string quartets, the *Silhouettes* and other piano pieces. The Dumka of the String Quartet in E flat op. 51 was read whole by measurement, and the scherzo of the seventh symphony for its metre; each technique marked **[score]** says where. The Slavonic Dances and the *Dumky* Trio are not in the catalogue.

## Techniques

1. **A dumka goes suddenly from melancholy to high spirits and back.** The word is a small *duma*, a brooding ballad. Usually slow and dreamy in two; the lively part breaks in. [Wikipedia, Dumka]
2. **One keynote, and the mode flips.** The quartet's Dumka: slow in G minor, quick in G major, slow in G minor, the slow tune once in G major, then a Presto in G minor. The keynote never moves. **[score: op. 51, second movement]**
3. **Slow in two, quick in three-eight.** *Andante con moto*, 2/4; *Vivace*, 3/8; and *Presto*, 3/8, to close. **[score: op. 51]**
4. **The quick part is longer than the slow part it interrupts.** 87 bars slow, 123 quick; then 20 and 22 slow, 50 quick. At the file's speeds the slow parts still last longer. **[score: op. 51]**
5. **A long note thrown across the bar line.** In the *Vivace* the commonest bar of the first violin is two eighths and a quarter that begins on the last eighth and runs into the next bar (31 such quarters in 123 bars). Next commonest: two sixteenths and an eighth; three eighths. **[score: op. 51]**
6. **The furiant's count: 2+2+2+3+3.** A fast Bohemian dance in three, whose accents make three groups of two out of the first two bars, then two plain bars. [Wikipedia, Furiant]
   *How:* in 3/8, a unit of four bars: accent the first, third and fifth eighths of the first pair of bars, then the first eighth of each of the next two.
7. **How the dance is laid out** (the *Vivace*'s first sixteen bars): the two violins together in one rhythm, the viola in sixteenths, the cello in steady eighths. **[score: op. 51]**
8. **The speed gives and takes.** *A tempo* again and again in the slow part; *poco meno mosso* and *poco più mosso* inside the quick one; a slowing over six bars before the slow part returns. **[score: op. 51]**
9. **The last quick part is the fastest and in the minor.** **[score: op. 51]**
10. **Where his furiants are:** Slavonic Dances op. 46 nos. 1 and 8; the scherzo of the sixth symphony (bars of 3/4 and 3/2 by turns); the third movements of the seventh and ninth symphonies; the last movement of the violin concerto, whose middle is a dumka. [Wikipedia, Furiant; Wikipedia, Dumka] The seventh's is written in 6/4, where two threes and three twos fill the same bar. **[score: Symphony no. 7, third movement]**
11. **Where his dumkas are:** op. 35 and op. 12 for piano, the sextet op. 48, the quartet op. 51, the piano quintet op. 81, three of the Slavonic Dances, and the *Dumky* Trio op. 90, six movements that are each one. [Wikipedia, Dumka]

## Walks through whole works

**Op. 51, second movement, "Dumka"** (302 bars). *Andante con moto*, 2/4, G minor, bars 1 to 87, the first violin's bars mostly three or four eighths, or an eighth and a long note. *Vivace*, 3/8, G major, bars 88 to 210, with its long notes across the bar, slowing from bar 187. *Andante con moto* again, G minor, bars 211 to 230; the same in G major, 231 to 252. *Presto*, 3/8, G minor, bars 253 to 302.

## What he does not do

*In this movement.*

- Change keynote between the slow and the quick.
- Let the quick part end the first time: it slows into the return.
