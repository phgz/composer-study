## Techniques

1. **Establish the accompaniment before the tune.** The low figure sounds alone before the upper line enters.
   *Works on:* form, texture.
   *Why:* the listener is given the accompaniment's timing before the melody complicates it. This is a structural inference from the order of entries, not a report of listening. **[score: Berceuse op.57]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* begin with a short, distinctive accompaniment; let the melody enter into an already established movement.
   *Where:* op.57, bars 1–3. **[score: Berceuse op.57]**
2. **A stable low anchor beneath changing upper detail.** The low D-flat attacks at the beginning of almost every bar in this reading; the right hand changes rhythm, register and number of simultaneous lines.
   *Works on:* harmony, melody, texture.
   *Why:* upper activity changes without requiring the low anchor to follow it. **[score: Berceuse op.57]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* keep an accompaniment recognizable while letting the melody grow an inner voice, widen and subdivide; retain its important melodic arrivals within the ornament.
   *Where:* op.57, opening through the variation sequence; the low-note positions and upper attack counts are in the dated record. **[score: Berceuse op.57]**
3. **Elaboration expands and then clears.** Activity is not an uninterrupted acceleration: busy spans give way to less crowded ones.
   *Works on:* form, rhythm.
   *How:* make more than one wave of elaboration, and after the densest passage recover a line whose individual notes have room.
   *Where:* op.57, upper attack counts in bars 19–30 and 39–50. These are descriptive windows, not asserted variation boundaries. **[score: Berceuse op.57]**

## What he does not do

In this work, increased melodic activity does not require increased harmonic speed in the accompanying low figure.
