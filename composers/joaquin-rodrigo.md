# Joaquín Rodrigo (1901-1999)

## Techniques

1. **A cor anglais sings over a guitar.** The Adagio's slow, quiet melody is given first by the cor anglais over a soft accompaniment of guitar and strings. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
2. **The melody gathers ornaments as it goes,** the guitar adding flamenco turns to it. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
3. **The tune comes back low on the guitar,** on its lowest strings, over quiet, strummed, dissonant chords: a sobbing voice. [Steinberg]
4. **The guitar talks with single winds and the horn** before its solo. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
5. **The solo (cadenza) goes from intimate to dancing:** quiet at first, then flamenco dance rhythms, then harp-like broken chords, then full strumming at the top of the instrument. The orchestra answers briefly and breaks off. [Steinberg]
6. **Then the tune, loud, by everyone:** the strings and full orchestra, *molto appassionato*, the woodwinds accompanying. It is the one climax. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
7. **A quiet close in the major** after a minor movement, on a calm broken chord of the guitar; the background, not the guitar's last note, brings the resolution. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
8. **Six-eight against three-four.** The first movement opens with the guitar alone strumming a three-bar figure in 6/8 whose accents fall as if in 3/4; the top note of its chord rises with each repeat. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
9. **Two and three mixed.** The last movement alternates bars of 2/4 and 3/4: phrases of nine beats (one bar of three, three of two). He called it a courtly dance. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
10. **Mostly woodwinds.** The orchestra is light: winds in pairs, two horns, two trumpets, plucked and bouncing strings, no percussion; one oboist takes the cor anglais. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
11. **Old Spain, flamenco and the eighteenth century at once:** Spanish-Italian guitar music of the 1700s, flamenco gesture, and a neoclassical frame; cross-rhythms and placed dissonances that recall Soler. [Steinberg]
12. **The *Adagio* for winds is A B A B A.** A: quiet; a lyrical melody passed among the upper woodwinds in solos and duets, a flute solo among them. B: like a fanfare, dark and driving, the timpani leading the brass in over a repeated rhythm. It closes quietly, with low instruments and timpani. Nine minutes. [Old Dominion note; Rodrigo's site]
13. **What he said the concerto holds:** the scents, birdsong and fountains of the gardens of Aranjuez. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]

## Numbers

*Concierto de Aranjuez*: three movements, in D major, B minor and D major; about 6, 13 and 5 minutes. He made a version for harp for Nicanor Zabaleta in 1974. *Adagio* for winds: piccolo, two flutes, three oboes, three clarinets, a bassoon, four horns, three trumpets, three trombones, tuba, timpani and percussion; first played in Pittsburgh in June 1966 by the American Wind Symphony under Robert Boudreau, who had asked for it.

## What he does not do

- Use percussion in the concerto.
- Let the guitar have the last word: the orchestra resolves under it.
