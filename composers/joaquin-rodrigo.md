# Joaquín Rodrigo (1901-1999)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026, from the sources at the end, read that day. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two slow pieces: the Adagio of the *Concierto de Aranjuez* (1939) and the *Adagio para orquesta de instrumentos de viento* (1966); with the outer movements of the concerto for their rhythm. **Scores:** none in score-reader's catalogue (all in copyright), so nothing below was checked against his notes.

## Techniques

1. **A cor anglais sings over a guitar.** The Adagio's slow, quiet melody is given first by the cor anglais over a soft accompaniment of guitar and strings.
   *Works on:* texture, colour.
   *How:* give a slow, quiet tune first to a low reed (the cor anglais) over the guitar's soft strummed chords and quiet strings.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio, the opening. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
2. **The melody gathers ornaments as it goes,** the guitar adding flamenco turns to it.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *How:* at each return of the tune, give it more ornaments: turns, quick runs and grace notes around its main notes.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio, the guitar's statements of the tune. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
3. **The tune comes back low on the guitar,** on its lowest strings, over quiet, strummed, dissonant chords: a sobbing voice.
   *Works on:* harmony, texture, colour.
   *How:* play the tune again low, on the lowest strings, with quiet strummed chords that clash with it.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio; the source gives no bar. [Steinberg]
4. **The guitar talks with single winds and the horn** before its solo.
   *Works on:* texture, colour.
   *How:* let single winds and a horn answer the guitar in turn, a phrase each, before it plays alone.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio, before the cadenza. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
5. **The solo (cadenza) goes from intimate to dancing:** quiet at first, then flamenco dance rhythms, then harp-like broken chords, then full strumming at the top of the instrument. The orchestra answers briefly and breaks off.
   *Works on:* rhythm, form.
   *How:* a solo passage in four stages: quiet; dance rhythms; broken chords running up the instrument; full strumming high. Let the orchestra answer for a moment and stop.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio, the cadenza. [Steinberg]
6. **Then the tune, loud, by everyone:** the strings and full orchestra, *molto appassionato*, the woodwinds accompanying. It is the one climax.
   *Works on:* form, loudness, colour.
   *How:* after the solo, give the tune to everyone, loud and with passion, the strings on the tune and the woodwinds accompanying; make it the only climax of the movement.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio, after the cadenza. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
7. **A quiet close in the major** after a minor movement, on a calm broken chord of the guitar; the background, not the guitar's last note, brings the resolution.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *How:* end a minor movement in the major, quietly: the guitar plays a calm broken chord, and the other instruments, not the guitar, sound the resolving chord.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, Adagio, the close. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
8. **Six-eight against three-four.** The first movement opens with the guitar alone strumming a three-bar figure in 6/8 whose accents fall as if in 3/4; the top note of its chord rises with each repeat.
   *Works on:* rhythm.
   *How:* a strummed figure in 6/8 (two threes) whose accents fall in groups of two (three-four), repeated, with the top note of the chord higher at each repeat.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, first movement, the opening (guitar alone). [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
9. **Two and three mixed.** The last movement alternates bars of 2/4 and 3/4: phrases of nine beats (one bar of three, three of two). He called it a courtly dance.
   *Works on:* rhythm, form.
   *How:* write phrases of nine beats: one bar of three, then three bars of two, and keep that pattern.
   *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, third movement. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
10. **Mostly woodwinds.** The orchestra is light: winds in pairs, two horns, two trumpets, plucked and bouncing strings, no percussion; one oboist takes the cor anglais.
    *Works on:* colour.
    *How:* keep the orchestra light: woodwinds in pairs, two horns, two trumpets, strings mostly plucked or bowed short, no percussion, so that a guitar can be heard.
    *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, the scoring. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez; Steinberg]
11. **Old Spain, flamenco and the eighteenth century at once:** Spanish-Italian guitar music of the 1700s, flamenco gesture, and a neoclassical frame; cross-rhythms and placed dissonances that recall Soler.
    *Works on:* harmony, rhythm.
    *How:* frame the music in clear eighteenth-century forms and phrases, and colour it with flamenco gestures, cross-rhythms and single, placed dissonances.
    *Where:* *Concierto de Aranjuez*, all three movements. [Steinberg]
12. **The *Adagio* for winds is A B A B A.** A: quiet; a lyrical melody passed among the upper woodwinds in solos and duets, a flute solo among them. B: like a fanfare, dark and driving, the timpani leading the brass in over a repeated rhythm. It closes quietly, with low instruments and timpani. Nine minutes.
    *Works on:* form.
    *How:* alternate a quiet, lyrical section (a tune passed among the high woodwinds, solo and in pairs) with a dark, driving one (timpani on a repeated rhythm, then the brass), five sections in all, ending quietly and low.
    *Where:* *Adagio para orquesta de instrumentos de viento* (1966), as a whole. [Old Dominion note; Rodrigo's site]
13. **What he said the concerto holds:** the scents, birdsong and fountains of the gardens of Aranjuez.
    *Works on:* background.
    *How:* not a technique: what he meant the concerto to call up, which may guide its colours.
    *Where:* his words on the *Concierto de Aranjuez*, as reported. [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]

## Numbers

*Concierto de Aranjuez*: three movements, in D major, B minor and D major; about 6, 13 and 5 minutes. He made a version for harp for Nicanor Zabaleta in 1974. *Adagio* for winds: piccolo, two flutes, three oboes, three clarinets, a bassoon, four horns, three trumpets, three trombones, tuba, timpani and percussion; first played in Pittsburgh in June 1966 by the American Wind Symphony under Robert Boudreau, who had asked for it.

## What he does not do

- Use percussion in the concerto.
- Let the guitar have the last word: the orchestra resolves under it.

## Too well known to borrow

- **The Adagio's melody** from the *Concierto de Aranjuez*: known through many adaptations, among them Miles Davis's *Sketches of Spain*, Chick Corea's "Spain" (which uses its opening), chart singles and films (*Brassed Off*, *School of Rock*). A low reed with a slow tune in the minor over a strummed guitar calls it up at once (composer-blend's piece 66). [Wikipedia, Aranjuez]
