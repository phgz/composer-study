## Techniques

### The variation finale of op. 67

1. **A quiet theme in two voices that move together, over a running middle voice.** The two violins play the theme in step with each other, mostly a third or a sixth apart, in even eighths, *p* and *pp*; under them the viola runs in triplet sixteenths, arpeggios of the chord. It is in B flat, in 2/4.
   *Works on:* melody, texture.
   *How:* give a short tune to two voices together, a third apart, in plain notes, softly; let a middle voice run underneath in quick broken chords.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, bars 1 to 8. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

2. **The theme's head filled in by step, twice as fast, in a middle voice, under plucked chords.** From bar 11 the viola plays in sixteenths a line that starts like the theme (a note said twice, then up to the fourth) with the steps between filled in, while the first violin plucks chords.
   *Works on:* melody, texture, colour.
   *How:* take the first notes of the tune, fill the leaps with passing notes, play them twice as fast in an inner voice, and pluck the chords above.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, bars 11 to 18 (two four-bar units alike). **[score: op. 67 finale]**

3. **The running figure passes from voice to voice, and quickens.** Section by section the part that runs changes, and its notes get shorter: sixteenths in all four (bars 23 to 38); six notes to the beat high in the first violin, the others in eighths (39 to 50); six to the beat in the three lower parts (51 to 65).
   *Works on:* rhythm, texture, form.
   *How:* keep the tune's harmony and outline, and at each variation give the quick notes to another instrument, faster than before.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, bars 23 to 65. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

4. **The middle variations in the keys of the lowered third and sixth.** From B flat the music moves to D flat (bar 66) and then G flat (bar 80), and comes back to B flat for the last part (bar 94).
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *How:* after the first variations in the home key, put the next ones in the key a minor third up, then in the key a major third down from home; return home for the end.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, key signatures at bars 66, 80, 94. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

5. **The farthest key is the barest.** The G flat section has the fewest notes of the middle variations (three or four a bar in each part, where the sections before it have five to ten), *molto dolce*, the cello plucked, the second violin mostly in quarter notes.
   *Works on:* texture, loudness, form.
   *How:* where the variations have travelled farthest from home, write the barest and softest of them.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, bars 80 to 93. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

6. **Twice as fast, in another metre, and the first movement comes back.** At bar 94 (*Doppio movimento*, 6/8, B flat again) the viola plays the first movement's opening figure (down a third, up a fourth, down a step, down a third, up a sixth: the intervals of its first bars) under arpeggios rushing down the first violin. The figure comes back in the parts through this section (to bar 118).
   *Works on:* melody, form, rhythm.
   *Why:* an analysis says the movement unifies the work with a theme related to the first movement's opening theme. [Hollywood Bowl] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* near the end of the variations, double the speed, change the metre to that of an earlier movement, and bring that movement's theme back in a middle voice.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, bars 94 to 121; first movement, bars 3 to 4. **[score: op. 67 finale; op. 67 first movement]**

7. **The two themes at once, three against two.** At bar 150 the metre is 2/4 again; the first violin plays the variation theme's head in notes twice as long, while the viola plays the first movement's figure in triplets against it (bars 151 to 153, in D flat). At the end, in B flat, the first violin has the theme in long notes again and the second violin the first movement's figure in triplets (bars 214 to 217).
   *Works on:* melody, rhythm, form.
   *Why:* the same unifying aim as technique 6. [Hollywood Bowl] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* put the variation theme in long notes on top and another theme in triplets under it, in the same harmony; let the two rhythms rub, three against two.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, bars 150 to 160 and 214 to 224. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

8. **Quiet, with a late swell.** The marks are mostly *p*, *pp* and *dolce*; at bar 162 *cresc. poco a poco* begins the long growth of the coda, which ends *f*.
   *Works on:* loudness, form.
   *How:* keep the variations soft and let the loudness grow only once, slowly, in the coda.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, the marks; bars 162 to 224. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

9. **Variations of different lengths.** Measured between the changes of key and metre, the sections are 65, 14, 14, 28, 28 and 75 bars: the variations do not keep the theme's length.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* not a recipe on its own: the theme's outline stays, but a variation may be shorter or twice as long.
   *Where:* op. 67, finale, the key and metre changes. **[score: op. 67 finale]**

### What the sources add

10. **The viola.** In this quartet the viola leads: the melody of the third movement is mainly the viola's, and Brahms joked to a friend that there was "no cello solo" but a viola solo so tender one might change instrument for it. In the finale's opening pages too the viola is prominent before it recedes into the ensemble.
    *Works on:* colour, background.
    *How:* not a technique on its own: it says the middle voice may carry the music.
    *Where:* op. 67, third movement and the finale's opening. [Wikipedia, String Quartet No. 3; Hollywood Bowl]

11. **Developing variation.** Schoenberg named his principle "developing variation": a motif transformed, in its intervals and its harmony, to build a whole work.
    *Works on:* background.
    *How:* not a technique on its own: it names the habit behind 2, 3 and 6, the theme's notes changed rather than repeated.
    *Where:* his music in general. [Wikipedia, Johannes Brahms]

## Walks through whole works

**String Quartet no. 3, op. 67, finale** (*Poco Allegretto con Variazioni*, 224 bars, by its sections of key and metre; an analysis counts eight variations [Hollywood Bowl], which were not marked out here). B flat, 2/4, bars 1 to 65: the theme in the two violins over the viola's triplets; the viola's quick version of its head under plucked chords; then the running notes passed between the parts and getting faster. D flat, 66 to 79. G flat, 80 to 93, the barest. B flat, 6/8, *Doppio movimento*, 94 to 121: the first movement's theme in the viola. D flat, still 6/8, 122 to 149. 2/4 again, 150 to 160: the two themes together, three against two. B flat, 161 to 224: the coda, growing from *cresc. poco a poco* to *f*, the two themes together once more.
