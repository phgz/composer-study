# John Rutter (born 1945)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two things: the carol, and the solo instrument that sings in his Requiem; and, for piece 83, what is said of his melody. **Scores:** none read. His music is under copyright; this page is from six sources, and what they say is said here in other words.

## Techniques

1. **A carol was a ring dance,** and he links the form to dancing in a circle.
   *Works on:* rhythm, form.
   *How:* give a carol a steady dance pulse and a refrain that comes round, as a circle of dancers comes round.
   *Where:* his account of the word in the interview, not a work. [NPR]
2. **A carol is a dance or a lullaby:** he says carols usually have one quality or the other.
   *Works on:* rhythm, form.
   *How:* decide which before writing: a dance (a lively pulse, a refrain) or a lullaby (a rocking pulse, soft); the same tune can be turned from one to the other.
   *Where:* his account in the interview. [NPR]
3. **A short introduction that comes back between the verses** as a bridge.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* write a few bars for the accompaniment alone, and play them again between each verse and the next.
   *Where:* *Shepherd's Pipe Carol*, the piano's introduction and its returns. [blog]
4. **A verse as a dialogue:** the lower voices, then everyone; the next verse laid out the same way.
   *Works on:* texture, form.
   *How:* give the first half of a verse to the lower voices alone and the second half to all; lay out every verse the same way.
   *Where:* *Shepherd's Pipe Carol*, the first two verses (tenors and basses, then the full choir). [blog]
5. **A reflective middle,** sweet and smooth, in the high voices; then a joyful end. The carol goes from piping to musing to celebrating.
   *Works on:* form, loudness.
   *How:* after two lively verses, a quieter, smooth middle for the high voices alone; then the lively music back, brighter, to end.
   *Where:* *Shepherd's Pipe Carol*: the sopranos' middle, marked sweet and smooth, then the close. [blog]
6. **Many metres close together,** and an accompaniment that must be soft, exact and off the beat.
   *Works on:* rhythm.
   *How:* change the metre often (a bar of three among bars of two, and so on) and give the accompaniment light, off-beat figures played softly and exactly.
   *Where:* *Shepherd's Pipe Carol*, the piano part. [blog]
7. **Mostly one plain major key, with catchy chromatic touches** and brief slips of the harmony to the side and back.
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *How:* stay in one major key; now and then let a chord slide to a neighbouring one a half step away and come back at once, and colour a note of the tune with a chromatic neighbour.
   *Where:* *Shepherd's Pipe Carol*, in F major. [blog]
8. **The pipe:** the carol's picture is a boy playing his pipe on the road; he heard such piping as a boy singer in Menotti's *Amahl and the Night Visitors*.
   *Works on:* melody, colour.
   *How:* give a high wind (flute or piccolo, or a high piano line) a short piping figure that recurs, as the boy's pipe on the road.
   *Where:* *Shepherd's Pipe Carol*, the subject; the sources name no bar of piping. [Wikipedia, Shepherd's Pipe Carol]
9. **Tunes that sing and do not leap:** in the Requiem's gentle movements the lines are in the scale and avoid wide skips; the harmony is modern but not harsh.
   *Works on:* melody.
   *Why:* lines that amateur choirs can sing, and a serene mood that dissonance would break. [Kálló] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* keep a gentle movement's tune in the scale, mostly by step, with no leap wider than a fifth; use chords with added notes but no harsh clashes.
   *Where:* Requiem, *Pie Jesu*, *Lux aeterna* and *The Lord is my shepherd*. [Kálló]
10. **A small cell worked:** the soprano's tune in the *Pie Jesu* grows from a fifth, which is then turned upside down and later widened to an octave.
    *Works on:* melody.
    *Why:* widening the fifth to an octave opens the voice's range and gives the tune room to move. [Kálló] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* begin a tune with one interval; answer it with the same interval turned upside down; later widen it (a fifth to an octave) at the high point.
    *Where:* Requiem, *Pie Jesu*, the soprano's opening and its returns. [Kálló]
11. **A solo instrument per movement:** a cello opens *Out of the deep* and the voices take up its figures in unison, low; an oboe in *The Lord is my shepherd*; a harp sets the *Pie Jesu*. In the *Pie Jesu* one voice sings over very little and the choir only echoes the ends of the phrases.
    *Works on:* texture, colour.
    *Why:* each solo sets its movement's mood: the oboe's singable tune the hope of *The Lord is my shepherd*, the harp the calm beyond words of the *Pie Jesu*. [Kálló] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* give each movement one solo instrument that leads or sets it; let one voice sing over very little, and the choir echo only the ends of its phrases.
    *Where:* Requiem: *Out of the deep* (cello), *The Lord is my shepherd* (oboe), *Pie Jesu* (harp and soprano). [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]
12. **A small band:** the Requiem's first version is for flute, oboe, timpani, glockenspiel, harp, cello and organ.
    *Works on:* colour.
    *How:* a small band of single players: one flute, one oboe, one cello, harp, glockenspiel, timpani and organ, each one able to be heard alone.
    *Where:* Requiem, the first version (1985). [Wikipedia, Requiem]
13. **Bells for joy:** the *Sanctus* is bright, with bells, flute and oboe, a flow of sixteenths in harp and organ, voices imitating one another, and a close where the entries crowd together.
    *Works on:* rhythm, texture, colour.
    *Why:* the bright scoring makes the movement lively and exclamatory, like the seraphim of Isaiah 6; the running sixteenths keep it moving to its loud end. It is the work's jubilant centre. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* for a joyful movement: bells, flute and oboe on top, an unbroken flow of sixteenths in harp and organ, voices entering one after another on the same figure, and closer and closer together at the end.
    *Where:* Requiem, *Sanctus*, ending on "Hosanna". [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]
14. **A steady beat on one note:** the Requiem opens over the timpani's quarter notes in three, and the *Agnus Dei* returns to a pulse on a single note.
    *Works on:* rhythm.
    *Why:* the same pulse ties the opening to the *Agnus Dei*, the two dark movements. [Kálló] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* begin over a single low note struck on every beat, slowly, and let other instruments enter over it before any key is clear; bring the pulse back later as a single repeated note.
    *Where:* Requiem, the opening of *Requiem aeternam* (the timpani; the voices enter in bar 7) and the *Agnus Dei*. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]
15. **The end is the beginning:** the last movement brings back the first movement's theme; the whole is an arch around the *Sanctus*.
    *Works on:* form.
    *Why:* the work closes in a circle, from tension to peace. [Kálló] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* plan the movements as an arch, pairing the first with the last and so inward around a middle; end on the opening's theme.
    *Where:* Requiem: *Requiem aeternam* and *Lux aeterna* share material; the *Sanctus* is the middle. [Kálló]
16. **A sudden far key by renaming one note:** in the *Pie Jesu*, from G minor to D flat major.
    *Works on:* harmony.
    *Why:* a sudden opening, which lets the soprano rise to a high A flat. [Kálló] *(an analysis)*
    *How:* hold a note and call it by another name (F sharp as G flat), and step straight into the far key that the new name belongs to.
    *Where:* Requiem, *Pie Jesu*, around bars 27 and 28. [Kálló]
17. **Where he comes from:** the French and English choral music of the early twentieth century, light music, and the American song.
    *Works on:* background.
    *How:* not a technique: it says which manners his are close to (French and English choral music of the early twentieth century, light music, American songwriting), so that a piece can tell what in it is his.
    *Where:* the Wikipedia article on him. [Wikipedia, Rutter]

### Dancing Day, read for piece 77 on 25 November 2026

18. **The harp gets the floor to itself.** In this cycle for upper voices and harp, the harp plays the prelude and, between groups of carols, an interlude.
    *Works on:* colour, texture, form.
    *How:* introduce the voices' world with their own accompanying instrument alone and make room for that instrument again between vocal sections.
    *Where:* *Dancing Day*, the opening prelude and the interlude between *Personent Hodie* and *There is no rose*. [Rutter catalogue]

### His melody and its dress, read for piece 83 on 27 November 2026

*From what he says and what is written about him; no score read.*

19. **A plain scale or arpeggio, dressed by what is set against it.** A magazine's portrait of his style: he calls himself half composer and half songwriter, a magpie rather than an explorer, and what makes his music his own is the way a simple scale or arpeggio is given a counter-line or a harmony.
    *Works on:* melody, harmony, texture.
    *How:* begin from a plain scale or a broken chord; make it new by what goes with it, a second line against it or a chord under it, not by changing the line.
    *Where:* his style in general. [BBC Music Magazine, "Rutter's style"]

20. **A tune that sounds as if it could not have gone otherwise.** In his words, a melody is best when it seems inevitable, as if it could not have been written any other way; and he calls himself more songwriter than composer, the gift of melody being what the concert world does not want.
    *Works on:* melody.
    *Why:* a melody should give a sense of being inevitable [J.W. Pepper, interview] *(his words)*
    *How:* try each note of a tune against the others it could have been; keep the one that seems the only one.
    *Where:* his tunes in general. [J.W. Pepper; Varsity]

## Numbers

**Requiem** (1985), seven movements in an arch around the *Sanctus*; the first and last share their material. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3. Pie Jesu | Andante e dolce | 3/4 | F major | soprano, choir |
| 4. Sanctus | Andante maestoso | 3/4 | C major | choir |

## What he does not do

*By these sources.*

- Harsh dissonance.
- The *Dies irae* in his Requiem: left out, as Fauré and Duruflé left it out.
- Tunes of wide leaps in the gentle movements. (*Out of the deep* is the exception: chromatic, with skips.)

## Too well known to borrow

- **The *Shepherd's Pipe Carol***: his first published piece, called his signature tune. [blog; Wikipedia, Shepherd's Pipe Carol]
