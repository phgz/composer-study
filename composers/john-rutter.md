# John Rutter (born 1945)

Begun by Claude on 23 November 2026. It grows each time he is used.

**Scope so far.** Two things: the carol, and the solo instrument that sings in his Requiem. **Scores:** none read. His music is under copyright; this page is from six sources, and what they say is said here in other words.

**Measured profile:** none: no work of his is in score-reader's catalogue.

**The pieces that used this page,** with Philip's words on each: composer-blend, [`reference/composers/john-rutter.md`](https://github.com/phgz/composer-blend/blob/main/reference/composers/john-rutter.md).

## Techniques

1. **A carol was a ring dance,** and he links the form to dancing in a circle. [NPR]
2. **A carol is a dance or a lullaby:** he says carols usually have one quality or the other. [NPR]
3. **A short introduction that comes back between the verses** as a bridge (*Shepherd's Pipe Carol*). [blog]
4. **A verse as a dialogue:** the lower voices, then everyone; the next verse laid out the same way. [blog]
5. **A reflective middle,** sweet and smooth, in the high voices; then a joyful end. The carol goes from piping to musing to celebrating. [blog]
6. **Many metres close together,** and an accompaniment that must be soft, exact and off the beat. [blog]
7. **Mostly one plain major key, with catchy chromatic touches** and brief slips of the harmony to the side and back. [blog]
8. **The pipe:** the carol's picture is a boy playing his pipe on the road; he heard such piping as a boy singer in Menotti's *Amahl and the Night Visitors*. [Wikipedia, Shepherd's Pipe Carol]
9. **Tunes that sing and do not leap:** in the Requiem's gentle movements the lines are in the scale and avoid wide skips; the harmony is modern but not harsh. [Kálló]
10. **A small cell worked:** the soprano's tune in the *Pie Jesu* grows from a fifth, which is then turned upside down and later widened to an octave. [Kálló]
11. **A solo instrument per movement:** a cello opens *Out of the deep* and the voices take up its figures in unison, low; an oboe in *The Lord is my shepherd*; a harp sets the *Pie Jesu*. In the *Pie Jesu* one voice sings over very little and the choir only echoes the ends of the phrases. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]
12. **A small band:** the Requiem's first version is for flute, oboe, timpani, glockenspiel, harp, cello and organ. [Wikipedia, Requiem]
13. **Bells for joy:** the *Sanctus* is bright, with bells, flute and oboe, a flow of sixteenths in harp and organ, voices imitating one another, and a close where the entries crowd together. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]
14. **A steady beat on one note:** the Requiem opens over the timpani's quarter notes in three, and the *Agnus Dei* returns to a pulse on a single note. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]
15. **The end is the beginning:** the last movement brings back the first movement's theme; the whole is an arch around the *Sanctus*. [Kálló]
16. **A sudden far key by renaming one note:** in the *Pie Jesu*, from G minor to D flat major. [Kálló]
17. **Where he comes from:** the French and English choral music of the early twentieth century, light music, and the American song. [Wikipedia, Rutter]

## Numbers

**Requiem** (1985), seven movements in an arch around the *Sanctus*; the first and last share their material. [Wikipedia, Requiem; Kálló]

| | speed marked | metre | key | singing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Requiem aeternam | Slow and solemn | 3/4 | C minor, then G major | choir |
| 2. Out of the deep | Slow, with some rubato | 4/4 | C minor | choir; a cello opens |
| 3. Pie Jesu | Andante e dolce | 3/4 | F major | soprano, choir |
| 4. Sanctus | Andante maestoso | 3/4 | C major | choir |
| 5. Agnus Dei | Slow and solemn | 4/4 | C minor | choir |
| 6. The Lord is my shepherd | Slow but flowing | 2/4 | C major | choir; an oboe |
| 7. Lux aeterna | Moderato (a quarter at 92) | 4/4 | C minor, then G major | soprano, choir |

Forces: the first version for flute, oboe, timpani, glockenspiel, harp, cello and organ; the second for two flutes, oboe, two clarinets, bassoon, two horns, timpani, glockenspiel, harp and strings. [Wikipedia, Requiem]

## What he does not do

*By these sources.*

- Harsh dissonance.
- The *Dies irae* in his Requiem: left out, as Fauré and Duruflé left it out.
- Tunes of wide leaps in the gentle movements. (*Out of the deep* is the exception: chromatic, with skips.)
