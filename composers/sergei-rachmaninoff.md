## Techniques

1. **The toll: three low notes, one to a beat, each in three octaves.** The C sharp minor prelude opens with A, G sharp, C sharp: down a semitone, then down a fifth to the keynote, the lowest note of the piano's bottom octave. **[score: op. 3 no. 2]**
2. **The three notes go on underneath as the bass,** over and over: thirteen times on those same notes, seventeen with the same shape moved to other notes. **[score: op. 3 no. 2]**
3. **Each low note is answered off the beat by a thick chord higher up:** stroke on the beat, six-note chord on the half-beat, three notes to a hand, in the middle of the keyboard. The chords' top line moves by step, mostly downward. **[score: op. 3 no. 2]**
4. **When it comes back it is the same music, thicker:** the chords have eight notes, twice eleven, and it is loud; then it dies away on soft chords of the home key. The middle is fast (three times the speed) in running triplets. **[score: op. 3 no. 2]**
5. **The peal: high notes coming down by step, each alternating with one fixed pair of notes.** In the B flat prelude the right hand has A, G, F in octaves, and between each of them the same B flat and D: bells of different sizes against one that does not change. Before it, a short fanfare: a quick upbeat and a chord. **[score: op. 23 no. 2]**
6. **Under the peal the bass rolls:** a low octave on the beat, then a broken chord up two and a half octaves and down again, six notes to the beat, every beat. **[score: op. 23 no. 2]**
7. **The small bell that swings:** in the B minor prelude, between slow chords low and middle, two pairs of notes rock in a long-short rhythm, the same two pairs again and again. **[score: op. 32 no. 10]**
8. **His tunes move by step and circle a few notes,** in the manner of church chant; several preludes each favour one interval (fifths, thirds, seconds, fourths). [Frolova-Walker]
9. **The Dies irae,** the chant for the dead, comes all through his work as a sign of death; in the B minor prelude it is not quoted but nearly. [Frolova-Walker]
10. **How the bells he heard are rung** (not his technique, his model): they are not tuned to a scale and play no melody; the ringing is rhythm and the mix of colours. The big bell tolls slowly and alone to call (three slow strokes, then steadier ones). For the dead, each bell is struck once from the smallest to the largest, each stroke left to fade, and then all together. For a feast, all the bells ring at once, in a pattern said three times. [Wikipedia, bell ringing]

## Walks through whole works

**Prelude in C sharp minor op. 3 no. 2** (61 bars, 4/4). From the score; the speeds are the edition's.
- **Bars 1 to 13, slow:** the toll (A, G sharp, C sharp, each in three octaves), then each stroke answered off the beat by six-note chords whose top line steps down (techniques 1 to 3).
- **Bars 14 to 42, fast** (three times the speed): running triplets.
- **Bars 43 to 61, slow again:** the opening, thicker (chords of eight notes, twice eleven) and loud, then dying away on soft chords of the home key. The three notes come thirteen times in the bass over the whole prelude, in bars 1 to 19 and 46 to 53 (technique 4).

## What he does not do

*In these three.*

- Leave the bass out: there is a low note or a low chord in nearly every bar.
- Put the answering chords on the beat with the low strokes.
- Decorate the toll: the three notes are bare octaves.
