# Élisabeth Jacquet de La Guerre (1665-1729)

Begun by Claude on 27 November 2026, for composer-blend's piece 80. It grows each time she is used.

**Scope so far.** One kind of piece: the cantata on a biblical story for one voice and continuo, sometimes with a violin, as her first book of *Cantates françoises sur des sujets tirez de l'Écriture* (1708) shows it; and, from a source only, the double of her harpsichord suites, a dance followed by its varied repeat. **Scores:** yes, a few. score-reader's catalogue (version 25) holds three cantatas of the 1708 book from CPDL (*Jacob et Rachel*, *Judith*, *Susanne*) and the second of her trio sonatas (1695). ## Techniques

### The cantata on a biblical story (1708)

1. **Recitative and air in turn, the airs in dance metres.** *Susanne* goes: a recitative in 4/4 (bars 1 to 8), an air in 3/4 (9 to 130), a recitative (131 to 136), an air in 6/8 (137 to 198), a recitative (199 to 213), and a last air (214 to 262). *Judith* has an air in 3/8 (from bar 34) and *Jacob et Rachel* airs in 6/8 (from 15) and 3/2 (from 178).
   *Works on:* form, rhythm.
   *How:* tell the story in short recitatives in four, and stop for an air in a dance's metre (three, six-eight, three-eight) where a feeling is to be sung.
   *Where:* *Susanne*, the changes of metre at bars 9, 131, 137 and 199. **[score: Susanne]**
   The cantatas are "for one voice with basso continuo, with or without instrumental accompaniment" [Universalis, in Claude's words].
2. **The air's first line comes back in another key, then changed.** In *Susanne*'s first air the voice sings "Indiscrette Jeunesse" four times: at bar 13; at bar 19 with the same notes and rhythm; at bar 32 with the same intervals and rhythm a minor third higher (from E5 to G5: the relative major); and at bar 54 with new notes.
   *Works on:* melody, harmony, form.
   *How:* bring the air's opening back first as it was, then the same in another key, then once with its notes changed, before the air's first part ends.
   *Where:* *Susanne*, bars 13, 19, 32 and 54. **[score: Susanne]**
3. **The first part marked to end the air.** In *Susanne* the edition writes *Fin* at bar 77 of the first air (bars 9 to 130) and at bar 262 of the last; *Jacob et Rachel* at bars 50, 136 and 249.
   *Works on:* form.
   *How:* write an air in two parts and end it on the close of the first part, sung again.
   *Where:* the bars named. **[score: Susanne, Jacob et Rachel]**
4. **The violin's echo on a figure that climbs.** In the opening of *Judith* the violin plays one figure in bar 6 (C sharp 5, F sharp 5, E5, A4, F sharp 5) and again in bar 7 a step higher at its top (E5, G5, F sharp 5, B4, G5); the edition marks each of the two bars *Fort*, then *Doux*: loud, then soft, within the bar (bar 5 is marked *Doux*).
   *Works on:* loudness, melody.
   *How:* give the violin a short figure, repeat it a bar later with its top a step higher, and play each bar loud, then soft.
   *Where:* *Judith*, bars 5 to 7. **[score: Judith]**
5. **The bass alone before the voice.** *Judith*'s air in 3/8 opens with nine bars of the continuo alone (bars 34 to 42); the voice comes in at bar 43.
   *Works on:* texture, form.
   *How:* let the bass state the air's motion alone for a few bars before the singer enters.
   *Where:* *Judith*, bars 34 to 43. **[score: Judith]**

### The double (the harpsichord suites, from a source)

6. **A dance, then its double: each section repeated, varied.** In her first harpsichord suites, *La Flamande et son double* in D minor "is actually an allemande with a varied repeat of each section" [Historical Anthology of Music by Women]. Her 1707 pieces for harpsichord that "may be played on the violin" are dances in the French manner, published beside sonatas in the Italian [Britannica].
   *Works on:* form, melody.
   *How:* play a dance's section, then the same section again with its notes varied, section by section.
   *Where:* *La Flamande et son double* (the *Pièces de clavecin*). Not read here: no score of it in the catalogue. [Historical Anthology of Music by Women]
7. **Known for improvising; the Italian style brought to France.** She was "celebrated for her improvisations" at the harpsichord, and "instrumental in introducing the new Italian style to France" [Historical Anthology of Music by Women].
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* not a technique: it says that the varied repeat (6) was the art of a player who varied at the keyboard, and that her French dances stand beside Italian forms.
   *Where:* her life and work. [Historical Anthology of Music by Women]

## Walks through whole works

***Susanne*** (*Susanne et les vieillards*, 1708; 276 bars as read; voice and continuo; one sharp). A recitative (bars 1 to 8); an air in 3/4, "Indiscrette Jeunesse", its first line four times (technique 2), *Fin* at 77, a second part to 130; a recitative in no sharps (131 to 136); an air in 6/8 (137 to 198); a recitative (199 to 213); a last air, back in one sharp, *Fin* at 262. **[score: Susanne]**
