## Techniques

### Repeating cells (*Shaker Loops*, *Phrygian Gates*)

1. **Loops of strings shaking between notes.** *Shaker Loops*, first for a string septet and then for string orchestra, keeps the idea of repeating loops of oscillations on the strings; its title is for the "shaking" of the strings as they oscillate between notes, and for the Shakers dancing to repetitive, energetic music. It was first called *Wavemaker* and meant to imitate the ripples of water.
   *Works on:* texture, rhythm, colour.
   *How:* give each string part a short cell that oscillates between two notes and repeats; let the parts' cells overlap like ripples.
   *Where:* *Shaker Loops*. [Wikipedia, Shaker Loops]
2. **Repeating cells that build large shapes.** He used "the fabric of continually repeating cells to forge large architectonic shapes".
   *Works on:* form, texture.
   *Why:* the cells' repetition is the material from which the large shapes are built. [Wikipedia, John Adams] *(his words)*
   *How:* make the long form out of the cells themselves, by how many play and for how long, not out of new themes.
   *Where:* *Shaker Loops*. [Wikipedia, John Adams]
3. **Gates: a square wave between two modes.** *Phrygian Gates* (25 to 30 minutes) is built of repeating cells and goes round the circle of fifths through half the keys: A Lydian, A Phrygian, E Lydian, E Phrygian, and so on, each key bright (Lydian) then dark (Phrygian); the time spent in the Lydian gets shorter as the piece goes on. The gates are named after the electronic gates of synthesizers, a term for switching quickly between states.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *Why:* he describes it as "in the form of a modulating square wave", one state Lydian and the other Phrygian. [Wikipedia, Phrygian Gates] *(his words)*
   *How:* hold a keynote and switch its scale between the Lydian (raised fourth) and the Phrygian (lowered second, third, sixth, seventh), back and forth; move the keynote round the circle of fifths; make the bright state shorter each time.
   *Where:* *Phrygian Gates*. [Wikipedia, Phrygian Gates]
4. **A steady pulse,** as the other minimalists of his time, to define and control the music.
   *Works on:* rhythm.
   *How:* keep one even pulse under everything.
   *Where:* his music of these years. [Wikipedia, John Adams]
5. **Minimalism with the climaxes of late Romanticism:** developed from Reich and Glass, but more ready to build the large orchestral textures and climaxes of Wagner, Mahler and Sibelius; repeating patterns without strict adherence to the movement.
   *Works on:* form, loudness, background.
   *How:* repeat and pulse as the minimalists do, but let the music swell to a climax.
   *Where:* his music in general. [Wikipedia, John Adams]
6. **Tonality as a force of nature:** his "diatonic conversion", from working with synthesizers; *Harmonielehre* called "a statement of belief in the power of tonality".
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* not a technique: what he believes of tonality.
   *Where:* [Wikipedia, John Adams]

## Walks through whole works

***Phrygian Gates***, as the article gives it: A Lydian, A Phrygian, E Lydian, E Phrygian, and on round the circle of fifths through half the keys, the Lydian parts shortening. [Wikipedia, Phrygian Gates]

***Shaker Loops***: four movements, *Shaking and Trembling*, *Hymning Slews*, *Loops and Verses*, *A Final Shaking*. [Wikipedia, Shaker Loops]
