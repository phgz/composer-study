## Techniques

### The prelude to *Tristan und Isolde*

1. **A short phrase climbs by half steps to a dominant seventh, and stops.** After an upbeat and a leap up of a sixth, the top line rises by half steps, four notes, and the phrase ends on a dominant seventh chord. Silence follows (three and a half beats in the reading), not the chord it points to.
   *Works on:* melody, harmony, form.
   *Why:* the opening chord moves on to the dominant seventh rather than to its nearest consonance, and the home chord that seventh points to does not come: a silence stands in its place. [Wikipedia, Tristan chord] **[score: Tristan prelude]** *(an analysis; from the score)*
   *How:* write a phrase of a few notes that rises by half steps and ends on a dominant seventh; follow it with silence, not with the chord it leads to. Do not use his opening chord or his notes (below, "Too well known to borrow").
   *Where:* bars 1 to 3. **[score: Tristan prelude]**

2. **The same phrase again, higher, with the silences shortening.** The first three statements end on dominant sevenths a third apart (on E, bar 3; on G, bar 7; on B, bar 11); the silences after them last three and a half, two, then one beat in the reading.
   *Works on:* form, harmony.
   *Why:* the three statements are heard each on a different pitch level, and the outer notes of the three rising lines spell the first chord. [UT Austin, Tristan and Isolde] *(an analysis)*
   *How:* say the stopped phrase again a third higher, then higher again, and shorten the silence each time, so the next phrase comes sooner.
   *Where:* bars 1 to 11. **[score: Tristan prelude]**

3. **One half step said over and over, then the first arrival is a wrong one.** Before the first arrival the top line says F to F sharp four times, an octave apart by turns (bars 13 to 16), climbs on, and the dominant seventh goes to the chord on the sixth degree, not to the keynote (bars 16 to 17: in A minor, the E seventh goes to F).
   *Works on:* harmony, melody, form.
   *How:* before a cadence, repeat a half step in alternating octaves; then let the dominant seventh go to the chord a step above the keynote's (the deceptive cadence).
   *Where:* bars 13 to 17. **[score: Tristan prelude]**

4. **Dominants that seldom go home.** In the prelude's 110 bars, of the 32 dominant chords followed by another chord of their key, 3 go to the keynote's chord (bars 24, 65, 107), 4 to the chord on the sixth degree (bars 16, 65, 73, 93), and 25 elsewhere. In the *Meistersinger* prelude, 40 of 132 go home.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *Why:* the prelude reaches its keynote's chord after a dominant three times in 110 bars: everywhere else the expectation a dominant sets up is left open. **[score: Tristan prelude, Meistersinger prelude]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* in a long passage let only about one dominant in ten reach the keynote; send the others to the sixth degree or to another dominant.
   *Where:* the whole prelude. **[score: Tristan prelude]**

5. **The dominant seventh as the commonest chord.** Of the analysts' 359 chord labels, 120 are dominant sevenths, 45 diminished sevenths and 28 half-diminished; plain major and minor chords together are 129.
   *Works on:* harmony.
   *How:* make the chord that points somewhere more common than the chord that has arrived.
   *Where:* the whole prelude. **[score: Tristan prelude]**

### The prelude to *Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg*

7. **A march that keeps arriving home.** In its 220 bars, 40 of the 132 dominant chords followed in their key go to the keynote's chord (in *Tristan*, 3 of 32). The analysts mark eight keys in turn, C, G, C, E, A, C, E flat, C; the last seventy bars (151 to 220) stay in C.
   *Works on:* harmony, form.
   *Why:* against the *Tristan* prelude, the same composer's other way: the dominant leads home about a third of the time, and the piece ends in a long stretch of its home key. **[score: Meistersinger prelude, Tristan prelude]** *(from the score)*
   *How:* let every second or third dominant reach home; travel through a few keys a third apart, then stay home for the last third of the piece.
   *Where:* the whole prelude; bars 151 to 220 in C. **[score: Meistersinger prelude]**

8. **Chords changing inside the beat, over a bass that moves by step.** The analysts mark 38 chords in the first eight bars, about five a bar (*Tristan*: about three); the bass moves by step 54% of the time. In bar 3 the bass falls G, F, E, D while the tune rises A, B, C, D.
   *Works on:* harmony, texture.
   *How:* under a march tune, change the chord on many beats and half beats, with the bass walking by step, often against the tune.
   *Where:* bars 1 to 8; bar 3. **[score: Meistersinger prelude]**

9. **A guild of singers, and a chorale in four parts.** The mastersingers are a guild of amateur poets and musicians, mostly master craftsmen, with an intricate system of rules for making and performing songs. The prelude takes its music from two episodes of the third act; there, the townspeople sing a chorale-like setting in four parts.
   *Works on:* background.
   *How:* not a technique on its own: it says that the march is the music of a guild of singers, and a four-part chorale belongs to it.
   *Where:* the opera; Act 3. [Wikipedia, Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg]

### The Wesendonck songs, measured

6. **Uneven rhythm and unexpected notes.** Against 1,083 Romantic songs: a note as long as the one before only 16% of the time against 40%; dotted figures 27% against 15%; the melody's next note harder to guess (3.14 bits against 2.43) and its range wider (13.6 half steps against 11.9).
   *Works on:* rhythm, melody.
   *How:* vary the lengths from note to note, dot them often, and let the line go where the listener does not expect.
   *Where:* the five songs; for one of his commonest figures, *Träume*, bar 18. **[score: the profile]**

## Walks through whole works

**Prelude to *Tristan und Isolde*** (110 bars; the analysts mark four keys in it, A minor, A major, A minor, and one more). Bars 1 to 11: the stopped phrase three times, rising, with shorter silences. Bars 13 to 17: the half step said over and over, and the first arrival a deceptive one. Through the rest: dominants that do not go home (technique 4). **[score: Tristan prelude]**

## What he does not do

*In the Tristan prelude.*

- Follow a dominant with its keynote's chord more than three times. **[score]**

## Too well known to borrow

- **The Tristan chord and the opening phrase it belongs to:** much written about, and well enough known to have been parodied and quoted by later musicians [Wikipedia, Tristan chord]. A piece takes the shape of technique 1, never these notes, in any key.
