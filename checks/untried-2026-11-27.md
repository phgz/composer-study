## Candidates

### A. A tune carried by loudness alone

- **The idea:** the notes stay where they are (one held chord, or one note repeated) and the tune is in the loudness: each "note" of the tune is one of the held notes brought forward, or the same note made louder or softer, so that the line's rise and fall is a rise and fall of loudness.
- **The nearest found:** Schoenberg's *Farben* (op. 16 no. 3, 1909), where "a standing chord" is "translated into a klangfarbenmelodie through the restless orchestration" (Wikipedia, Klangfarbenmelodie): the same idea with colour, not loudness. Messiaen's *Mode de valeurs et d'intensités* (1949) gives "each pitch of the same name" its own "duration, attack and intensity for each register" (Wikipedia, Quatre études de rythme): loudness tied to pitch, but no tune carried by loudness.
- **That it can be heard:** McDermott, Lehr and Oxenham (2008) found that "contour representations are also produced by patterns in loudness and brightness", and that loudness and brightness "are nearly as useful as pitch contours for recognizing familiar melodies".
- **Not found** in the two searches made for it (a melody made by dynamics alone on a held chord; "Dynamikmelodie", "melody of dynamics"); two more found the nearest, above.
- **What could go wrong:** recorded instruments change colour with loudness only as far as their recorded layers go, and the renderer's `DYNAMIC_RESPONSE` has not been tried on a piece; the steps must be large to be heard (in 67 Philip heard shaped lines as "subtle to my ear, but noticeable"). It is close to *Farben* in kind: a listener who knows *Farben* may hear it as its loudness version, which may be all it is.

### B. A harmony changed by balance alone

- **The idea:** one set of pitches held throughout (for example C, E, G and B), the chord heard changing with which of them are loud: C, E and G loud is C major with a B in it; E, G and B loud is E minor over a soft C. A progression with no note changing.
- **The nearest found:** *Farben* again, its harmony moved by colour; static harmony in general ("the sustained tonic chord elaborated only by surface voice leading", harmony.org.uk).
- **Not found** in two searches (a progression made by changing the relative loudness of held notes; harmony changing without the notes changing).
- **What could go wrong:** whether loudness alone makes a listener hear a different root is not shown by anything read here; the room and the instruments' own balance may blur it.

### C. A canon in loudness

- **The idea:** a second voice copies the first's swells and fades a bar later, on other notes: the imitation is in the loudness, not the pitches.
- **Not found** in one search; nothing near it found either. Weaker than A and B: one search.
