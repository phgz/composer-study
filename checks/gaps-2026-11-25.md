It was put off at sixteen pages. There are now 34. Philip, 25 November 2026: "We can try to tackle that now, and do it again later."

**What was run:** `python3 -m study.gaps --blend ../composer-blend`. It reads the 34 pages (497 numbered entries) and composer-blend's "Tried" lines, and writes the lists in [`gaps-2026-11-25/lists.md`](gaps-2026-11-25/lists.md):
1. what the techniques work on, and how often two dimensions meet in one technique;
2. each page's techniques that no piece has taken;
3. every *Why*, by dimension;
4. every page's "What he does not do".

The lists are counted; what follows is Claude's reading of them.

**Three cautions:**
- **The tags are Claude's.** Every "Works on" line was written by Claude, so an empty cell can be a habit of tagging as well as a gap in the music.
- **A gap on these pages is a gap in what the project has read and made, not in music.** Hocket, for one, is centuries old (not checked against a source here). The pages are the composers the draws brought, and each one covers one or two kinds of piece.
- **A gap can be empty because it does not work,** and one piece will not show which (composer-blend's procedure, Part 1, I).

## What the lists show

1. **Loudness never acts on a tune.**
   - Of 127 techniques that work on melody, none works on loudness too.
   - Loudness has 34 techniques. 23 of them work on form as well: where the loud place is, how a piece fades or ends.
   - Loudness meets harmony in 3 techniques and rhythm in 2.
   - Only Beethoven's 4 ("Each line swells") and 12 ("Every bar swells and fades, under the breath") shape loudness inside a phrase, and both are about breathing, not about which note matters.

2. **Purposes reached only one way.** Read from the *Why* lines:
   - **Moving the metre's weight is always done by rhythm.** Dvořák 5 (a long note across the bar line) and 6 (the furiant's 2+2+2+3+3, which "the dancer's jumps and stamps follow"), Duruflé 2 (chant's twos and threes), Tavener 9 (no metre), Rodrigo 9 (two and three mixed). No page does it by colour, loudness or harmony.
   - **A floor that does not move, so that everything that goes somewhere happens above it, is always a low pitch.** Borodin 4, Buxtehude 1, Rameau 1, Rachmaninoff 2, Tavener 1, Duruflé 6, Whitacre 18, Dvořák 2. The fixed thing on top has been made in a piece (71: the voice keeps her D while the fugue's entries climb), so this one is not open.
   - **Terror and the supernatural are made by a scale.** Glière 4 (whole-tone), Rimsky-Korsakov 18 (whole-tone, low), Desplat 7 (a raised fourth and a lowered second, on the organ). Rameau's stage storms (17 to 20) make violence by rhythm and texture, but his page gives no *Why* for them and no piece has taken them.

3. **What no piece has taken.**
   - Most untaken: Price 11 of 41, Meredith Monk 10 of 17, Rameau 10 of 20, Rimsky-Korsakov 10 of 24, Glière 7 of 20, Julia Wolfe 6 of 16.
   - Most of those are there because the page grew after the piece.
   - Among them is a cluster on rhythm made by bodies and by passing:
     - Monk 12, a self-hocket, then a hocket between singers;
     - Monk 13, rapid syllables traded between groups;
     - Monk 16, accents that shift phase, tapped by feet;
     - Wolfe 5, a rhythm stamped out by a player;
     - Price 34, a short figure handed quickly from one wind to the next.
   - Two cannot be made without what has not been checked: Wolfe 8 needs sung words, and Monk 14 needs sounds beyond singing. Whether any installed voice can do either was not looked at.

4. **A habit, seen on the way.** Bells are the matter of pieces 67, 68 and 68b, and the piano tolls them in 74b: four of the nineteen pieces from 60 on. Four pages give a reason for bells (Rachmaninoff 1 and 5, Rimsky-Korsakov 13, Rutter 13, Tavener 20). Being available is not a reason to use them again.

## Three candidates

Each one is a gap, techniques on the pages at its edge, a meeting place of two composers, and how they would act on each other. None is a cast: the route and the draw stay Philip's. The procedure's route from pages leaves out the composers of the last four pieces (72 to 74b), which takes out Monk, Wolfe and Glière unless he chooses them.

### A. The metre carried by colour: the count in the feet

- **The gap:** the metre's weight moved by colour, not by rhythm (2 above), and the untaken cluster on rhythm made by bodies (3 above).
- **The meeting place:** Monk 16 (accents that shift phase, tapped by feet; untaken) and Dvořák 6 (the furiant's count, whose accents "the dancer's jumps and stamps follow"; taken in 65). Two worlds, one concrete thing: an irregular accent made by the body.
- **How they would act on each other:**
  - A tune in plain, even bars. Its notes are handed from instrument to instrument on the furiant's count: Monk 12 (a hocket) and Price 34 (handed on, joined on a shared note) for the passing, Rimsky-Korsakov 8 (a phrase passed between instruments; taken) for which colours join.
  - So the furiant is heard at first only in the colour, never in the notes.
  - Under it, stamps (Wolfe 5; untaken) shift phase against the count, a step each time round (Monk 16).
  - Where the stamps and the colours fall together on the count, the notes take it too: the dance arrives because the bodies brought it.
- **What could go wrong:**
  - A note handed on every few beats is exactly where Philip hears a glitch: "mostly a wrong-sounding note or a stumble in the rhythm".
  - The renderer's instruments speak at different delays (in 66, 15 to 37 thousandths of a second), so each hand-over has to be asked for early by its own instrument's delay, and measured.
  - The stamps would come from the renderer's new Iowa found objects (`objects-kit`), not yet auditioned for this.

### B. Terror two ways: by scale and by rhythm

- **The gap:** terror and the supernatural are made on the pages by a scale (2 above). Rameau's storms are the one way by rhythm, and untaken.
- **The meeting place:** Rameau 19 (the furies: the top and the bass run in scales while the middle repeats; untaken) and 18 (the infernal air: short rushes that land on long notes; untaken), with Rimsky-Korsakov 18 (a grim motif low in the orchestra on a whole-tone scale; untaken). The concrete thing: the running scale as menace. Rameau's runs are in the mode; Rimsky-Korsakov's menace is the whole-tone scale itself.
- **How they would act on each other:**
  - The furies run in their mode. The whole-tone notes enter their scales one by one, until the run is the whole-tone scale.
  - Then the low motif, the whole-tone one, takes Rameau's rhythm: rushes that land on long notes.
  - The pitch of the one and the rhythm of the other end as one thing.
- **What could go wrong:**
  - The motif must be new: Rimsky-Korsakov's own (the Sultan's, in *Scheherazade*) is on his page's "Too well known to borrow".
  - Rameau's dances worked as a quick third (63: 4.25), but in 69 Philip heard him "alone during his parts". The convergence has to happen in the same bars, not in turns.
  - A storm made by repeated notes "as fast as it can" (Rameau 17) needs quick repeated notes from the renderer's strings. On Philip's hosted strings, piece 60 measured a short note as far louder than a held one at the same gain.

### C. Loudness inside the line

- **The gap:** loudness never acts on a tune (1 above).
- **At its edge:** Beethoven 12 (every bar swells and fades; taken in 74b) against Cage 12 (no swell, as a rule; taken in 64); Rimsky-Korsakov 7 (a swell made by adding groups; taken in 68).
- **The idea:** one tune whose notes never change, while each statement leans on a different note, so that the same notes mean something else each time. Or two colours that act on each other by loudness alone, one rising only where the other gives way.
- **What could go wrong:** recorded notes change colour with loudness only as far as their recorded layers go. The renderer's `DYNAMIC_RESPONSE` has not been tried on a piece. In 67, Philip heard shaped lines as "subtle to my ear, but noticeable". This may be an ingredient for a piece rather than a piece.

**Claude's order:** A, then B, then C. A brings together the most of what the lists found: a purpose reached one way, untaken techniques and a meeting of two worlds on one thing. Its risk is the one Philip hears most easily, which is a reason to try it as a short first section.
