# Three ears on piece 64b's inner voices (23 November 2026)

**The questions:**
1. How surprised are NotaGen (NotaGen-X, and the paper's checkpoint before reinforcement learning) and the Anticipatory Music Transformer (AMT) by the alto and tenor of piece 64b, given its tune and bass?
2. Would they have chosen the proposals we chose?
3. How does AMT's ear compare with ours and NotaGen's on the 29 held-out works of `notagen-ear-2026-11-23.md`?
4. What does AMT write, given 64b's tune and bass?

**Status:** the inputs are ready, and the scripts for the Mac are written. Nothing has been scored or written yet. The predictions below were written before.

## Why our own ear is not one of the three here

64b's inner voices were drawn from our continuations, and chosen only among the drawings whose surprise fell in the middle half of Weber's own. So our ear's bits on them are set by how they were made, not measured. The same holds for the proposals: all were drawn from our model, and all fall within its band. Our ear appears below only where that does not matter: its place for the chosen proposal, beside the others'.

So 64b is music none of the three has heard, but only NotaGen and AMT hear it as outsiders.

## The ears

- **NotaGen-X:** as in `notagen-ear-2026-11-23.md`, with the prompt "Romantic / Chopin, Frederic / Keyboard". Weber is not among its composers; 64b is a mazurka on Chopin and Rachmaninoff.
  - **The score it reads:** 64b's piano score in its own ABC, two staves. Tune and alto are above, tenor and bass below, in four voices; the doublings are chords.
  - **Key and spelling:** E minor, but E flat major in bars 89 to 104, each pitch spelt with sharps, or with flats from bar 85 to 104.
  - **What it has heard of the bar:** NotaGen writes each bar's voices in turn. So it hears a bar's tune before its alto, and its alto before its tenor, but not the bar's bass.
- **AMT Large and Medium** (`study/amt.py`; music-large-800k, 780M parameters; music-medium-800k, 360M):
  - **What it is asked about:** the inner voices. The tune and the bass, with their doublings, are given notes ("controls"): AMT reads each about 5 seconds before it sounds.
  - **What each inner note is heard after:** the inner notes before it, and the outer voices up to 5 seconds past it.
  - **Time:** in the seconds the renderer plays 64b at.
  - **A note's bits:** those of its pitch, given its onset and its length, on any instrument.
  - **What it doesn't see:** bars, key or spelling.

## The tests (`three-ears-64b-2026-11-23/`)

1. **64b's inner voices as chosen:** 647 notes (326 alto, 321 tenor), the same for each ear.
2. **The proposals:** 446, drawn for 76 segments of four bars (38 alto, 38 tenor), each heard as it was drawn.
   - **What a proposal is heard with:** the inner voices chosen before it, the tune and the bass. A tenor also has the alto chosen for its bars; AMT is given that alto, since it was known in full.
   - **The measure:** each ear's bits a note for each proposal. The chosen one's place among its segment's proposals goes from 0 (the least surprising) to 1 (the most), and chance is 0.5. The p comes from drawing each place at random.
   - **Agreement:** the median Spearman's rho between two ears, over a segment's proposals.
3. **AMT on the 29 held-out works:** the inner notes compared in `notagen-ear-2026-11-23.md`, the outer lines given.
   - **Tempo:** a quarter note of 90 a minute, since AMT needs seconds.
   - **Repeats:** where the reading's counted-once repeat left a silence, it is closed.
4. **AMT writing:** AMT's own `generate()` (top-p 0.98, seed 1) writes notes between 64b's tune and bass.
   - **What it writes:** bars 2 to 80 and 89 to 160; the inner voices are silent in the intermezzo, bars 81 to 88. It begins after bar 1's inner voices, which are Claude's.
   - **How it is played:** `amt64.py` plays 64b with AMT's notes in place of the alto and the tenor: those from middle C up at the alto's level, those below at the tenor's. Everything else is 64b's own: tune, bass, doublings, tempi, dynamics, pedal.

## Predictions, written before anything was scored (Claude's, not Philip's)

1. **AMT Large on the 29 works:** lower than ours on inner lines (2.32) but higher than NotaGen (1.16), between 1.5 and 2.3 bits a note.
2. **AMT Medium:** more surprised than AMT Large on those works.
3. **On 64b:** NotaGen and AMT are both more surprised by its inner voices than by the held-out works' inner lines.
4. **The choices:**
   - AMT Large places our chosen proposals better than chance: mean place under 0.5.
   - NotaGen's mean place is between 0.4 and 0.6.
   - The choices were made for each note's fit with the harmony planned under the tune and bass. AMT hears those both ways; NotaGen hears neither the bar's bass nor anything after it.
5. **Agreement:** the ears agree little over a segment's proposals, median rho under 0.4 for every pair.
6. **NotaGen before its reinforcement learning:** no prediction. Reinforcement learning toward a judge's preferences usually costs some likelihood on real music, but NotaGen-X also had an extra stage of training on classical scores, and the two pull opposite ways. (Added on 23 November, before this checkpoint was downloaded.)

## AMT's writing: the first run, and a trial before the next

**The first run (AMT Large, seed 1; `amt_large_write.json`, 23 November):** 27 notes in 245 seconds, 0.11 a second, where 64b's own inner voices have 2.6.
- None after the intermezzo, though the second span ran for 20 minutes: AMT wrote rests.
- 13 of the 27 repeat a pitch the tune or bass plays within a third of a second, often a whole chord of it a little early or late: an echo of the given part, not a voice of its own.
- Not rendered: there is nothing to hear.
- **Not lost in the code (checked on the Mac with AMT's own `generate(debug=True)`, 2 to 12 seconds, the first setup):** 9 of the 10 notes it sampled were rests, one a second; the tenth was the first run's first note (B4 at 7.0 s). So AMT chose the rests; `ops.unpad()` only drops them.

**Why, as far as can be told without running it (Claude's reading, not checked):** two things in how the task was set, each unlike AMT's own accompaniment example.
1. **Too little history:** AMT was given only bar 1's inner voices, six slow notes. Its example gives it 5 seconds of the real accompaniment first.
2. **One instrument for both parts:** the given notes and the written ones were all the piano. AMT's training asks for whole instruments given the others (and for single notes given random others, on any instrument). A given part that is a whole line on the same instrument as the part asked for is neither.

**The trial:** four runs of 12 seconds with AMT Large, crossing the two changes.
- **History:** bar 1's inner voices (`amt_write.json`), or bars 1 to 5 of 64b's own (`amt_write_long.json`, 19 notes, 8 seconds; writing from bar 6).
- **Given notes:** on the piano, or on the bright piano, program 1 (`--given-program 1`), the written ones staying the piano's.
- **For comparison:** 64b's own inner voices have 28 notes in the first trial's 12 seconds (2.33 a second) and 29 in the second's (2.42).

**The rule, written before the trial:**
- **The full writing, for both models, uses the setup that comes nearest to 64b's density with the fewest echoes.**
- **Nothing is forced:** if no setup writes at least one note a second, that is the finding. No rests are forbidden and no notes filtered.
- **What it changes:** with five bars of history, AMT writes bars 6 to 160, not 2 to 160.

**The trial's result (AMT Large, seed 1, 12 seconds each; `trial_*.json`):**

| History | Given on | Notes a second | Echoes |
|---|---|---|---|
| bar 1 | the piano | 0.08 (1 note) | 1 |
| bar 1 | the bright piano | 3.67 (44) | 4 |
| bars 1 to 5 | the piano | 0.08 (1) | 1 |
| bars 1 to 5 | the bright piano | 2.50 (30) | 0 |

- **The instrument was the cause:** with the given notes on the piano, AMT writes almost nothing, whatever its history. On another program it writes a part.
- **The choice, by the rule:** bars 1 to 5 as history, the given notes on the bright piano. It writes 2.50 a second against 64b's 2.42, with no echoes: two voices in quarter notes, E3 to A4, the inner voices' range.
- **The full writing, both models:** from bar 6 with that setup. The first run (`amt_large_write.json`) is kept as the record of the failure.

**AMT's ear, the same question:** `amt_inputs_two_pianos.jsonl` is `amt_inputs.jsonl` with the given notes on the bright piano. The asked notes are scored on any instrument either way. Scoring both says whether the instrument matters to what AMT expects of the inner voices.

**A fix to the inputs:** `amt_inputs.jsonl` now has the corrected tempo map past the piece's last onset. Before, the last notes of the 12 proposals for the last four bars (5-alto-3, 5-tenor-3) were 10 ms long; 18 tokens change. Scores made before this fix are scored again.

## Running it where the weights are (Philip's Mac)

In the NotaGen environment, with a clone of AMT's code (`git clone https://github.com/jthickstun/anticipation ~/anticipation`; the scripts read it from there and nothing needs installing). Then:

```bash
D=checks/three-ears-64b-2026-11-23
# 4a. the writing trial (AMT Large, four runs of 12 seconds): each prints its notes a second and its echoes
for h in amt_write amt_write_long; do for g in 0 1; do
  python study/amt_write.py ~/models/music-large-800k ~/anticipation $D/$h.json -o $D/trial_${h}_given$g.json --given-program $g --until 12
done; done
# 4b. AMT writing, in full, with the setup the trial chose: bars 1 to 5 as history, the given notes on the bright piano
python study/amt_write.py ~/models/music-large-800k ~/anticipation $D/amt_write_long.json -o $D/amt_large_write_long_given1.json --given-program 1 --seed 1
python study/amt_write.py ~/models/music-medium-800k ~/anticipation $D/amt_write_long.json -o $D/amt_medium_write_long_given1.json --given-program 1 --seed 1
# (the first run, kept as the record of its failure; not to be run again:)
#   python study/amt_write.py ~/models/music-large-800k ~/anticipation $D/amt_write.json -o $D/amt_large_write.json --seed 1
# 1-3. AMT's ear (476 inputs, minutes), the given notes on the piano, then on the bright piano
python study/amt_score.py ~/models/music-large-800k $D/amt_inputs.jsonl -o $D/amt_large_scores.jsonl
python study/amt_score.py ~/models/music-medium-800k $D/amt_inputs.jsonl -o $D/amt_medium_scores.jsonl
python study/amt_score.py ~/models/music-large-800k $D/amt_inputs_two_pianos.jsonl -o $D/amt_large_two_pianos_scores.jsonl
python study/amt_score.py ~/models/music-medium-800k $D/amt_inputs_two_pianos.jsonl -o $D/amt_medium_two_pianos_scores.jsonl
# 1-2. NotaGen's ear (448 windows of up to 1024 patches)
python study/notagen_score.py WEIGHTS $D/notagen_inputs.jsonl -o $D/notagen64_scores.jsonl --verify ~/NotaGen
python $D/compare64.py
```

**NotaGen before its reinforcement learning:** the paper's fine-tuned checkpoint (`FINETUNE` below, `weights_notagen_pretrain-finetune_p_size_16_p_length_1024_p_layers_c_layers_6_20_h_size_1280_lr_1e-05_batch_1.pth`, 6.19 GB).
- **Why:** NotaGen-X's last stage, reinforcement learning toward pieces a judge scores well, helps it write but bends its probabilities away from plain likelihood. This checkpoint is the model trained only to predict real scores: the cleaner ear.
- **Not just NotaGen-X minus a step:** X also had another stage of training and other data.
- **Its architecture is NotaGen-X's,** so the same scorer reads it:

```bash
E=checks/notagen-ear-2026-11-23
python study/notagen_score.py FINETUNE $E/inputs.jsonl -o $E/scores_finetune.jsonl --verify ~/NotaGen
python $E/compare.py $E/scores_finetune.jsonl            # writes compare_scores_finetune.json
python study/notagen_score.py FINETUNE $D/notagen_inputs.jsonl -o $D/notagen64_finetune_scores.jsonl
python $D/compare64.py                                     # with "NotaGen fine-tuned" beside the others
```
