# The critic against Philip's words: what it should find

Written on 22 November 2026, after the ear was saved (catalogue version 24 at 92bb2c2; "flat": the 8 notes around a note under
0.56 bits a note, what 5 in 100 spans of the catalogue's held-out music fall under) and before the critic was run on any piece.

1. **Piece 37b**, rated 3.5. Philip: "the main problem is Riley. It seems to just be a flat tune in the background that doesn't
   evolve." Riley's part is the pulse and the modules (parts `pulse`, `mar`, `harp`, `vib`, `bn`, `fl`, `cel`); the melody is the
   cello (`vc`, Bellini and Chopin). Expected: at least one of Riley's parts flat for 30 seconds or more; the cello not.
2. **Piece 37**, the first writing, rated 3.25 ("I hear a bit of Riley"): Riley's part is the same. Expected: as 37b.
3. **The pieces he rated 5 that can be read here, 27, 29 and 49, and 46 (4.75):** expected: the whole texture never flat for 8
   seconds or more.

Piece 38's Adams layer ("a bit flat" at first hearing; clearer on the second) is not a part of its own: Adams is the phrase, how
each sentence is built across the parts. It is not a test of the critic.

## What it found

Run the same day: `study/critic.py --ear` (the ear saved by `--build`) on the six pieces. All three expectations hold.

| Piece | Rating | Texture flat 8 s or more | Lines flat 30 s or more | The melody |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 37b | 3.5 | none | `pulse` 0:02-2:49 (167 s) | `vc` flat 0% of its time |
| 37 | 3.25 | none | `pulse` 0:02-2:49 (167 s); `mar` 1:31-2:05 (33 s) | `vc` 1% |
| 27 | 5 | none | none | |
| 29 | 5 | none | none | |
| 49 | 5 | none | none | |
| 46 | 4.75 | none | none | |

- **Riley's other parts in the first writing are flat more of the time:** the marimba 56%, the bassoon 45%. In the second writing they are 25% and 15%. That fits Philip on 37b: "the rest, it's already more interesting".
- **The texture is never flat in any of the six,** because something always moves. The useful warning is a line flat for 30 seconds.
- **What this shows:** a pulse of one note is the easiest thing to call flat, so the check shows that the critic sees what Philip heard, not that it would find a subtler flat layer.
